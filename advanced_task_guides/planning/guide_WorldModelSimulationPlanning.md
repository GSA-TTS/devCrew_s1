## Executive Summary

World-Model Simulation Planning is the task family of deciding what to do next by predicting consequences inside an explicit model of the environment before committing any action to that environment. The planner estimates a belief state, proposes candidate actions, simulates each forward, scores the resulting trajectories against utility and hard constraints, commits only the first bounded action of the winning trajectory, observes the real result, measures divergence between prediction and observation, and replans. This guide provides AI agents with a structured protocol for designing the predictive model before runtime and operating the simulate-commit-correct loop at runtime, bounded by measured model competence rather than by the fluency of its forecasts. It specifies the model contract, the trust envelope governing when simulation may authorise action, the rollout and scoring machinery, the divergence attribution taxonomy, the observability record, and the metrics determining whether simulation earns its cost. It emphasizes structured execution protocols, quality assurance checkpoints, and error handling procedures to ensure reproducible planning under uncertainty.

***

## I. Foundational Concepts and Definitions

### 1.1 Core Terminology

**World-Model Simulation Planning (WMSP)**: A planning pattern in which candidate actions are evaluated against predicted consequences generated by an explicit environment model, and only a bounded prefix of the selected trajectory is executed before re-observation. WMSP is not the practice of asking a generative model what might happen; it is a controlled loop with a declared state representation, action set, transition model, rollout policy, scoring rule, uncertainty treatment, execution boundary, and correction mechanism.

**World model**: Any explicit predictive representation answering what next state, observations, rewards, costs, and hazards are plausible given a state, an action, and context. Admissible forms include physics simulators, digital twins, learned latent dynamics models [9][10], probabilistic programs, rules engines, causal models, language models, and hybrids. A world model need not reconstruct the environment in full; it must predict the quantities whose accuracy would change the selected action [8].

**Belief state**: A decision-relevant representation of the environment derived from observations, carrying known facts, unresolved uncertainty, weighted hidden-state hypotheses, resource state, existing commitments, and provenance. A single guessed state is inadmissible where the environment is partially observable.

**Candidate**: A legal, feasible, meaningfully distinct next action proposed for simulation, including the null actions of no-op, defer, probe, and escalate.

**Rollout**: A simulated trajectory produced by applying a candidate action to the belief state and repeatedly predicting successor states under a rollout policy until a horizon, terminal state, hard failure, confidence floor, or compute budget terminates it. A rollout is a sample from a predicted distribution, never a report of fact.

**Rollout policy**: The action-selection rule applied at simulated steps beyond the first, named and versioned separately from the deployed policy.

**Trust envelope**: The declared region of state space, action classes, and horizon depths within which the world model has measured competence, together with the authority that competence grants. Per region, it states whether the model may authorise an action, may only advise a scorer, or may only generate hypotheses for tool-based or human verification.

**Execution boundary**: The enforced separation between operations that are simulations, operations that are read-only observations, and operations that cause real effects. Simulation tools must be technically incapable of invoking production side-effecting interfaces.

**Reality gap**: The aggregate discrepancy between the modelled and the actual environment, spanning numeric prediction error, omitted side effects, interface change, strategic behaviour by other parties, sensor failure, and institutional or ethical constraints absent from the model.

**Model exploitation**: The failure mode in which optimisation pressure discovers actions scoring well because of simulator imperfections rather than because they succeed in reality. Risk grows with search intensity, rollout depth, action novelty, and evaluator narrowness.

**Divergence**: The measured difference between a prediction and the observation that followed it, attributed to a named error class rather than recorded as undifferentiated plan failure.

**Decision run**: The bounded unit of WMSP work, opened with an objective and budgets, carrying one or more decision steps, and closed by success criteria, stop conditions, budget exhaustion, or escalation.

### 1.2 The Minimum Operating Model

A production WMSP system requires six distinguishable functions. One component may perform several, but each must be separately named, versioned, and measurable, because divergence attribution in Phase 6 depends on knowing which function produced an error.

**Table 1: The six functions of a minimum WMSP system**

| Function | Responsibility | Failure signature when absent |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **State estimation** | Converts observations into a typed belief state carrying uncertainty, hypotheses, provenance, and staleness | Coherent plans built on stale or prematurely collapsed state |
| **Candidate generation** | Proposes legal, feasible, distinct actions including no-op, defer, probe, and escalate | Strategic alternatives silently absent from the comparison |
| **Transition simulation** | Predicts next state, observations, reward, cost, and hazards per state-action pair as a distribution | A single polished narrative treated as the future |
| **Rollout control** | Sets breadth, depth, sample count, pruning, and computation allocation | Unbounded search through a biased model |
| **Evaluation and risk control** | Scores trajectories on utility, hard constraints, cost, latency, reversibility, uncertainty, tail risk, and information gain | Catastrophic tails averaged away inside a mean score |
| **Execution and learning** | Commits a bounded action, captures the observation, measures divergence, updates state and model, triggers replanning | Open-loop execution of an imagined trajectory |

**Separation of simulator and evaluator**: The component generating a future and the component scoring it must not be the same unchallenged instance, since correlated error and self-confirmation otherwise dominate the ranking. Separation is achieved through different prompts, model families, checkpoints, tool-backed validators, or independent review. It does not guarantee independence; it makes disagreement observable, and observable disagreement is the principal input to the trust gate in Step 3.3.

**Receding-horizon discipline**: WMSP optimises over a horizon but executes only the first action or the smallest safely atomic bundle, then re-observes and solves again [13]. This short commitment horizon is the pattern's strongest safeguard, converting a fallible long forecast into a sequence of locally verified decisions. Any design executing a full imagined trajectory without intermediate observation violates the pattern rather than optimising it.

### 1.3 Applicability Conditions and Exclusions

WMSP is warranted when at least four of the following hold simultaneously, and is presumptively unwarranted when fewer than three hold.

- A wrong action is irreversible, expensive, unsafe, reputation-damaging, or hard to undo, as with purchases, message sending, permission changes, deployments, physical motion, or machine control.
- Outcomes depend strongly on action ordering or delayed consequences, so a reactive one-step policy is myopic.
- Real trials are scarce or costly while simulated trials are cheaper, at a simulated-to-real cost ratio below 0.10 per evaluated candidate.
- The environment has repeatable dynamics that can be learned, encoded, or approximated within a validated envelope.
- Two or more plausible actions exist and the latency budget permits comparing them before acting.
- Post-action results are observable within the decision cycle, so model drift is detectable rather than latent.
- Safety depends on screening actions against rare severe outcomes rather than on optimising expected success alone.

WMSP must not be selected in the following situations, each an exclusion rather than a discouraged practice.

- The action is cheap, reversible, rapidly observable, and lower in risk than the cost of modelling it; direct action with verification is the correct pattern.
- No decision-relevant transition model can be validated, since fluent forecasting is not a substitute for measured predictive accuracy.
- The environment is chaotic, adversarial, novel, or fast-changing such that uncertainty expands faster than the planner can correct it. Rollouts deeper than one step are prohibited in this regime, though a one-step conservative loop with an abstain path may remain admissible.
- A deterministic workflow, hard-coded rule, database transaction, constraint solver, or human approval supplies a simpler and stronger guarantee.
- Latency budgets make deliberation more harmful than a timely safe response.
- The action requires external permission, legal review, safety certification, or human consent, since simulated success is never authorisation.
- The proposed actions lie outside the model's training or validation distribution, where optimisation produces plans that look excellent only because they exploit simulator error.
- The only available predictor is a general generative model with no task-specific transition validation, since narrative or visual plausibility is never evidence of decision fidelity [18].

Where model adequacy is uncertain rather than absent, agents must default to a short-horizon conservative loop with an explicit abstain path, and must switch to direct observation, a trusted tool, a human operator, or a different planning pattern when one-step predictions are unreliable in the relevant state region.

## II. Operational Framework: Bounded Lookahead With Measured Commitment

### Phase 1: Decision Framing and Model Contract Specification

**Objective**: Establish, before runtime, which outcomes must be predicted accurately and what the world model is contractually obliged to return, so that model selection follows the decision rather than preceding it.

#### Step 1.1: Define the Decision and the Outcomes Worth Predicting

**Required Actions**:

- Name the recurring decision as a choice among action classes rather than as a goal.
- Enumerate the outcomes whose accurate prediction would change which action is selected, discarding every outcome whose prediction would not.
- Assign each action class a consequence tier from 1 (fully reversible, locally scoped) to 4 (irreversible, externally visible, safety-relevant) using reversibility, blast radius, and cost of error.
- Record the decision cadence and the latency, compute, and real-action budgets.

**Required Outputs**:

- A decision statement naming the action classes under comparison.
- A decision-relevant outcome list of no more than 12 entries.
- A consequence-tier assignment covering every action class, with budgets attached.

**Quality Checkpoints**:

- Every retained outcome would change the action ranking if its predicted value moved by 1 standard deviation; outcomes failing this test are removed.
- Every action class carries exactly one consequence tier; unassigned classes block progression.
- The latency budget exceeds projected planning cost by a factor of at least 2, or the decision is re-scoped to a smaller candidate set.

***

#### Step 1.2: Specify the World-Model Contract

**Required Actions**:

- State what constitutes the current state and which variables are observed, inferred, hidden, or uncontrollable.
- Declare the action vocabulary and parameter schema using the parameter names carried by the execution interface.
- Declare the return obligations: next-state fields, event probabilities, costs, rewards, hazards, and an uncertainty measure per predicted quantity.
- Declare the valid time scale, the maximum horizon in steps and wall-clock time, the enumerated operating envelope with explicit exclusions, and the evidence grade permitting the model to authorise, advise, or hypothesise only.

**Model contract template**:

```
CONTRACT_ID:
STATE_DEFINITION: OBSERVED / INFERRED / HIDDEN / UNCONTROLLABLE VARIABLES
ACTION_SCHEMA: ACTION_CLASSES, PARAMETERS_PER_CLASS
RETURN_OBLIGATIONS: NEXT_STATE_FIELDS, EVENT_PROBABILITIES,
  COST_AND_REWARD_COMPONENTS, HAZARD_FLAGS, UNCERTAINTY_MEASURE
TEMPORAL_VALIDITY: STEP_DURATION, MAX_HORIZON_STEPS, MAX_HORIZON_WALLCLOCK
OPERATING_ENVELOPE: IN_SCOPE_REGIONS, IN_SCOPE_ACTION_CLASSES, EXPLICIT_EXCLUSIONS
AUTHORITY_GRADE: AUTHORISE_IF / ADVISE_IF / HYPOTHESISE_ONLY_IF
```

**Required Outputs**:

- A completed model contract in the template above.
- A parameter cross-reference mapping every contract action parameter to its execution-interface field.

**Quality Checkpoints**:

- Every decision-relevant outcome from Step 1.1 appears in the return obligations; coverage below 100% blocks model selection.
- Every action parameter maps to exactly one execution-interface field, since unmapped parameters are the root cause of binding errors in Step 5.3.
- The contract names at least one explicit exclusion, since an envelope with no stated exclusion has not been analysed.

***

#### Step 1.3: Select the Model Class and Uncertainty Representation

**Required Actions**:

- Match the model class to the environment: rules and workflow graphs where transitions are contractual, mechanistic simulators for known physical processes, digital twins where mechanistic models combine with asset-specific observations, learned observation models for high-dimensional perception, latent dynamics models for efficient imagination, and language models where states and actions are textual or interface-based.
- Ground any language-model component in retrieved documentation, demonstrations, tool schemas, interface snapshots, or execution traces rather than unaided generation.
- Select the smallest model class satisfying the contract, recording rejected larger alternatives and reasons.
- Represent uncertainty through an ensemble of at least 3 members, at least 5 stochastic samples, or at least 3 assumption variants, and define both the disagreement statistic and the out-of-distribution indicator.

**Required Outputs**:

- A model class selection record with rejected alternatives, reasons, and a grounding inventory of bound evidence sources.
- An uncertainty specification naming ensemble size, sample count, disagreement statistic, and out-of-distribution flag threshold.

**Quality Checkpoints**:

- The selected model predicts every contract return obligation; a model omitting any obligation is rejected regardless of accuracy elsewhere.
- Uncertainty derives from at least 3 independent sources of variation, since a single sample provides no dispersion signal.
- The out-of-distribution indicator flags at least 90% of a held-out set of deliberately out-of-envelope inputs, or it is redefined.

***

### Phase 2: Model Validation and Trust Envelope Construction

**Objective**: Measure predictive competence at the level that governs decisions, and convert those measurements into an enforceable trust envelope binding runtime authority.

#### Step 2.1: Validate at the Decision Level

**Required Actions**:

- Measure one-step transition accuracy against held-out real transitions, reported per decision-relevant field rather than as a single aggregate.
- Measure multi-step degradation by plotting predictive quality against rollout depth from 1 step to the contract maximum.
- Measure event calibration across at least 5 probability bins, constraint-prediction recall as the share of real violations predicted in advance, and action-ranking agreement against real or high-fidelity outcomes.
- Partition every measurement by state region, action class, horizon depth, and consequence tier.

**Required Outputs**:

- A per-field one-step accuracy record partitioned four ways.
- A horizon curve locating the depth at which quality falls below the decision threshold.
- Calibration, constraint-recall, and ranking-agreement records per consequence tier.

**Quality Checkpoints**:

- Constraint-prediction recall is at least 0.95 for tier 4 and 0.80 for tier 3; shortfalls remove those tiers from the envelope entirely.
- Event calibration error is at most 0.10 across all bins for tiers 3 and 4; larger error demotes the model to advisory authority.
- Where no degradation depth is found within the tested range, the maximum horizon is capped at the deepest tested value rather than extrapolated.
- Surface-level similarity is never reported as validation evidence in place of the five decision-level measures.

***

#### Step 2.2: Construct and Bind the Trust Envelope

**Required Actions**:

- Translate each validated partition into an envelope entry naming its state region, action classes, maximum horizon, and granted authority.
- Raise the accuracy floor by at least 0.10 for each consequence tier ascended, so irreversible actions face stricter evidence than reversible ones.
- Define the four runtime trust modes with the numeric conditions selecting each, binding them to enforcement so an out-of-envelope request cannot silently proceed in normal mode.
- Define the envelope contraction rule applied when runtime divergence appears.

**Table 2: Trust modes and their selection conditions**

| Mode | Entry condition | Runtime behaviour | Escape condition |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Normal simulation** | Accuracy at or above 0.85 in the region, calibration error at or below 0.10, disagreement at or below 0.15, no drift alert within 24 hours | Full horizon, standard sample count, standard scoring | Any entry condition fails |
| **Conservative simulation** | Accuracy 0.70 to 0.85, or disagreement 0.15 to 0.30, or a drift alert within 24 hours | Horizon halved, samples doubled, scoring on the 10th percentile outcome, approval gate for tiers 3 and 4 | Accuracy below 0.70 or disagreement above 0.30 |
| **Probe first** | A read-only observation exists whose expected uncertainty reduction is at least 0.20 at a cost of at most 0.05 of the decision's expected loss | Execute the probe, re-estimate state, re-enter the gate | No qualifying probe exists |
| **Abstain or hand off** | Accuracy below 0.70 for the region, or the action class is outside the envelope, or tier 4 with disagreement above 0.20 | Emit an abstention record, escalate to a human or trusted tool | Envelope re-validated for the region |

**Required Outputs**:

- A trust envelope table with one entry per validated partition and a per-tier accuracy floor schedule.
- An enforced binding rejecting out-of-envelope requests, and a contraction rule stated as a numeric trigger with its resulting envelope change.

**Quality Checkpoints**:

- Every contract action class is either inside an envelope entry or explicitly excluded; unaddressed classes are treated as excluded.
- The tier 4 accuracy floor exceeds the tier 1 floor by at least 0.20.
- Out-of-envelope requests are rejected in 100% of test-harness trials; any leakage blocks deployment.

***

#### Step 2.3: Establish the Execution Boundary and Simulation Isolation

**Required Actions**:

- Partition every available operation into simulation, read-only observation, or side-effecting execution, labelling each with exactly one partition.
- Enforce isolation so simulation components hold no credential or route reaching a side-effecting interface, and so simulated output is never rendered in the schema reserved for real tool responses.
- Provide safeguards for every side-effecting operation: idempotency keys, transaction boundaries, rate limits, dry-run modes, and rollback hooks where supported.
- Define the approval gate, naming the consequence tier and confidence conditions above which independent authorisation precedes execution.

**Required Outputs**:

- An operation partition register with one label per operation and an isolation attestation recording credential separation.
- A safeguard inventory per side-effecting operation and an approval gate specification with its triggering tier.

**Quality Checkpoints**:

- Zero operations carry more than one partition label, and zero operations are unlabelled.
- A penetration test in which a simulation component attempts a side-effecting call fails in 100% of attempts.
- Every tier 4 operation carries a rollback hook, an approval gate, or both.

***

### Phase 3: Run Initialization and Belief-State Estimation

**Objective**: Open a bounded decision run with frozen component versions, construct a defensible belief state, and determine through the trust gate which mode of planning the situation permits.

#### Step 3.1: Open a Decision Run

**Required Actions**:

- Record the objective, success criteria, hard constraints, risk appetite, budgets, planning horizon, required approvals, and stop conditions.
- Assign a unique run identifier, linking it to a parent run where the run originates from replanning.
- Freeze and record the versions of the world model, evaluator, rollout policy, candidate proposers, tool schemas, and prompt templates in use.
- Restate the execution boundary for this run, naming which specific operations are simulations, read-only, or side-effecting.

**Required Outputs**:

- A run header carrying identifier, objective, criteria, constraints, budgets, and stop conditions.
- A frozen version manifest and a per-run execution boundary statement.

**Quality Checkpoints**:

- Every budget is a number with a unit; unbounded budgets block the run from opening.
- The version manifest resolves every component to a specific identifier, since an unpinned component makes divergence attribution impossible.
- At least one stop condition is a hard limit reachable without success, so a run cannot execute indefinitely.

***

#### Step 3.2: Estimate the Current Belief State

**Required Actions**:

- Normalise the latest observations into the typed state declared in the model contract.
- Mark timestamps, source, provenance, missing fields, contradictory evidence, and which values are inferred rather than observed.
- Maintain weighted competing hypotheses where the state is partially observed, preserving at least the top 2 whenever the leading hypothesis weight is below 0.80.
- Compute a staleness figure per decision-relevant field and a distribution-shift score, refreshing any field exceeding its staleness bound.

**Required Outputs**:

- A typed belief state with provenance, missingness flags, and inferred-value markers.
- A hypothesis set with weights summing to 1, plus staleness and distribution-shift records.

**Quality Checkpoints**:

- No decision-relevant field exceeds a staleness of 10% of the shortest transition time constant in the environment; breach forces re-observation before planning.
- Where the leading hypothesis weight is below 0.80, at least 2 hypotheses survive into rollout generation.
- Contradictory evidence is recorded as contradiction rather than silently resolved by preferring the more recent observation.

***

#### Step 3.3: Run the Suitability and Trust Gate

**Required Actions**:

- Look up the current state region, candidate action classes, and required horizon against the envelope entries from Step 2.2.
- Evaluate the distribution-shift score, disagreement statistic, recent drift alerts, and consequence tier of the pending decision.
- Select exactly one trust mode using Table 2, applying its parameter changes to horizon, sample count, scoring rule, and approval requirements before any rollout is generated.
- Emit an abstention record with its reason where abstain mode is selected, routing the decision to the named fallback.

**Required Outputs**:

- A gate decision record naming the selected mode and every condition value producing it.
- Mode-adjusted rollout parameters, and an abstention or handoff record where applicable.

**Quality Checkpoints**:

- Exactly one mode is selected and the selection is reproducible from the recorded condition values.
- No decision proceeds in normal mode when any single entry condition in Table 2 fails.
- Tier 4 decisions with disagreement above 0.20 terminate in abstention or handoff in 100% of cases.
- The gate is evaluated before rollout generation, so an elaborate search cannot launder an invalid model into an apparently precise decision.

***

### Phase 4: Candidate Generation and Rollout Execution

**Objective**: Assemble a small, genuinely diverse action set, allocate simulation effort in proportion to closeness and consequence, and produce distributions of predicted futures rather than single narratives.

#### Step 4.1: Generate a Bounded, Diverse Candidate Set

**Required Actions**:

- Propose actions that are legal, feasible, relevant, and meaningfully distinct, adding at least one safe alternative from no-op, defer, request-information, probe, or escalation to every set.
- Draw tier 3 and 4 sets from multiple proposal methods: a learned or heuristic policy for likely actions, rules for mandatory actions, a planner for long-range alternatives, and an adversarial proposer for safer counterplans.
- Remove duplicates and any candidate violating a hard constraint before simulation, recording the rejection reason rather than discarding silently.
- Cap the set at 7 entries and floor it at 3, escalating to human review where more than 7 genuinely distinct candidates survive filtering.

**Required Outputs**:

- A candidate register with action class, parameters, proposer identity, and preconditions per entry.
- A rejection log naming each removed candidate and its reason.

**Quality Checkpoints**:

- The set contains between 3 and 7 entries and includes at least one safe alternative.
- Every retained pair differs in at least one decision-relevant outcome prediction; pairs that do not are merged.
- Tier 3 and 4 sets draw from at least 2 distinct proposal methods, since a single proposer hides the alternatives it does not generate.

***

#### Step 4.2: Allocate the Rollout Budget and Search Policy

**Required Actions**:

- Set breadth, depth, sample count, and participating ensemble members from the mode-adjusted parameters produced in Step 3.3.
- Allocate more computation where candidate scores are close, uncertainty is high, or the tier is 3 or 4, and less where one candidate dominates on every decision-relevant outcome or where marginal simulation value falls below the cost of delay.
- Select the search method to match branching structure: direct enumeration for small sets, beam search where several sequences must remain alive, tree search with selective expansion where branching is large, trajectory optimisation for continuous controls, and policy-guided sampling where a learned controller proposes continuations efficiently.
- Name the rollout policy used beyond the first simulated step and state the branch-pruning rule as a numeric condition rather than a discretionary judgement.

**Required Outputs**:

- A rollout budget record naming breadth, depth, samples, and ensemble members, with an allocation rationale.
- A search method selection with parameters, a named rollout policy, a numeric pruning rule, and a worst-case termination estimate.

**Quality Checkpoints**:

- Sample count is at least 5 per candidate for tiers 1 and 2 and at least 20 for tiers 3 and 4.
- Depth never exceeds the horizon located by the Step 2.1 curve; requests to exceed it are rejected rather than granted with a warning.
- Worst-case termination falls within the latency budget with at least 20% headroom, or the method is downgraded to a cheaper alternative.

***

#### Step 4.3: Generate Stochastic Rollouts

**Required Actions**:

- Apply each candidate first action to the belief state once per surviving hypothesis, then predict next state, observations, reward, cost, and hazards at each step as a distribution rather than a single trajectory.
- Propagate uncertainty forward, recording model confidence, ensemble disagreement, and out-of-distribution flags at every step.
- Select each subsequent simulated action using the named rollout policy.
- Terminate each branch at the horizon, a terminal state, a hard failure, a confidence floor, or budget exhaustion, recording which terminator fired.

**Rollout step protocol**:

```
FOR EACH CANDIDATE C, HYPOTHESIS H, SAMPLE S:
  STATE := H.STATE; ACTION := C.ACTION; DEPTH := 0
  WHILE DEPTH < MAX_DEPTH:
    PREDICT NEXT_STATE, EVENTS, REWARD, COST, HAZARDS
    RECORD CONFIDENCE, ENSEMBLE_DISAGREEMENT, OOD_FLAG
    IF HARD_FAILURE OR TERMINAL OR CONFIDENCE < FLOOR:
      TERMINATE BRANCH, RECORD TERMINATOR
    IF OOD_FLAG AND CONSEQUENCE_TIER >= 3:
      TRUNCATE BRANCH, RECORD OUT_OF_ENVELOPE
    STATE := NEXT_STATE; ACTION := ROLLOUT_POLICY(STATE); DEPTH := DEPTH + 1
  EMIT TRAJECTORY WITH PER_STEP UNCERTAINTY
```

**Required Outputs**:

- A trajectory set per candidate and hypothesis carrying per-step predicted state, events, rewards, costs, hazards, and uncertainty.
- A termination record per branch naming the terminator that fired.

**Quality Checkpoints**:

- Sample counts match the Step 4.2 budget; shortfalls invalidate that candidate's score rather than being tolerated.
- Every branch carries a recorded terminator, since a branch without one indicates an uncontrolled loop and halts the decision step.
- Depth beyond 3 steps is used only where the horizon curve demonstrates incremental predictive value at that depth.
- No trajectory is emitted without per-step uncertainty; uncertainty-free predictions are rejected at the interface.

***

### Phase 5: Adversarial Challenge, Scoring, and Bounded Commitment

**Objective**: Attack the predicted futures before ranking them, convert survivors into a risk-sensitive ordering, verify that the selected action binds correctly to the real interface, and commit the smallest safe unit of execution.

#### Step 5.1: Challenge and Admit Trajectories

**Required Actions**:

- Generate adverse but plausible transitions in addition to modal ones, covering at least the 10th percentile outcome for every candidate, and perturb uncertain inputs across their stated ranges.
- Swap model variants or ensemble members and compare the resulting candidate rankings, searching deliberately for the hidden event or omitted side effect that would reverse them.
- Test correlated failures for tiers 3 and 4 rather than assuming independence.
- Reject any trajectory crossing outside the supported region, depending on an unverifiable assumption, omitting a known side effect, or violating a hard constraint, truncating to the in-envelope prefix and logging the trigger.

**Required Outputs**:

- An adverse-scenario set per candidate and a ranking stability record across variants and perturbations.
- A counterexample list with plausibility estimates, and a correlated-failure analysis for tiers 3 and 4.
- A surviving trajectory set per candidate with a rejection and truncation log.

**Quality Checkpoints**:

- Every candidate carries at least one adverse trajectory; candidates evaluated only on their modal future are unranked.
- Rank inversion under variant swap forces conservative mode or abstention for tiers 3 and 4.
- Zero trajectories extend beyond the validated envelope, since truncation is preferred to invented precision in 100% of cases.
- Aggregate rejection above 40% returns the decision to the trust gate in Step 3.3, since widespread rejection indicates the mode selection was too permissive.

***

#### Step 5.2: Score Candidates Under Risk-Sensitive Rules

**Required Actions**:

- Score each candidate on success probability, expected utility, downside at the 10th percentile, constraint satisfaction, cost, latency, reversibility, information gain, and uncertainty.
- Apply the elimination sequence in order: remove candidates with predicted hard-constraint violations, then those whose catastrophic-loss probability exceeds the tier threshold, then penalise uncertainty, model disagreement, and unsupported extrapolation, then compare expected utility among survivors, then break near-ties in favour of reversibility, observability, and information gain.
- Keep hard vetoes structurally separate from soft preferences, so a veto can never be outweighed by a high utility score.
- Preserve every component score and the combination policy rather than emitting a single opaque number.

**Required Outputs**:

- A component score matrix with one row per candidate and one column per criterion.
- A veto register naming each eliminated candidate and the constraint that eliminated it.
- A ranked survivor list with the combination policy recorded alongside it.

**Quality Checkpoints**:

- Catastrophic-loss probability is at most 0.001 for tier 4, 0.01 for tier 3, and 0.05 for tiers 1 and 2; breach eliminates the candidate without appeal.
- No veto is expressible as a weight, since vetoes and weighted preferences occupy separate mechanisms.
- Candidates within 5% of the leader on expected utility are treated as tied and resolved by reversibility rather than by the utility ordering.

***

#### Step 5.3: Select, Verify, and Authorise One Real Action

**Required Actions**:

- Select the most robust survivor, defined as the candidate whose ranking holds across model variants and adverse scenarios, rather than the one with the highest mean simulated reward.
- Re-verify that the selected action's preconditions still hold against a freshly observed state.
- Perform an execution diff comparing the exact tool call, payload, target, amount, permissions, or motion about to be issued against the parameters that were simulated.
- Obtain and verify currency of any approval required by the Step 2.3 gate, preferring the safer reversible action or a probe where top candidates remain tied after robustness comparison.

**Required Outputs**:

- A selection record naming the chosen candidate and its robustness evidence.
- An execution diff report comparing simulated and actual parameters field by field.
- An approval record with authoriser identity and validity window where applicable.

**Quality Checkpoints**:

- The execution diff shows 100% field agreement; any mismatch halts execution and returns to Step 5.2.
- Preconditions are re-verified against an observation no older than the Step 3.2 staleness bound.
- Approval is present and unexpired for every tier 4 action; a missing or stale approval halts execution.
- Where the robust leader differs from the mean-reward leader, the divergence is recorded and the robust leader prevails.

***

#### Step 5.4: Execute Only the Committed Tranche

**Required Actions**:

- Execute the first action or the smallest safely atomic bundle, never the full imagined trajectory.
- Apply the safeguards registered in Step 2.3: idempotency key, transaction boundary, rate limit, dry-run where available, and rollback hook.
- Capture the exact executed payload hash, start and finish timestamps, and the raw tool or actuator response.
- Halt the run and enter recovery where the interface returns an error, a timeout, or a response whose schema does not match the expected form.

**Required Outputs**:

- An execution record with payload hash, idempotency key, timestamps, and raw response.
- A safeguard application record and an execution status of committed, failed, or indeterminate.

**Quality Checkpoints**:

- The committed tranche is 1 action, or a bundle whose members cannot be separated without leaving the environment inconsistent.
- Zero actions execute beyond the committed tranche before re-observation occurs.
- Every tier 3 and 4 execution carries an idempotency key or an equivalent replay guard.
- Indeterminate executions are treated as possibly committed and route to verification rather than to retry.

***

### Phase 6: Divergence Measurement, Model Update, and Replanning

**Objective**: Close the loop by measuring prediction against observation, attributing error to a specific failure class, adjusting trust, and deciding whether to continue or terminate the run.

#### Step 6.1: Observe and Attribute Divergence

**Required Actions**:

- Capture the actual next observation immediately after execution, before any further planning.
- Compare predicted and observed state changes, events, rewards, costs, constraint outcomes, and timing, computing a residual per decision-relevant field.
- Attribute each material divergence to exactly one class from the taxonomy below, since the correction depends entirely on the class.
- Flag residuals exceeding the 95th percentile of the validation residual distribution as anomalies requiring review before they enter any learning path.

**Table 3: Divergence attribution taxonomy**

| Class | Definition | Primary correction |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Observation error** | Sensing or extraction produced a wrong state reading | Repair the state estimator or extraction pipeline; do not retrain the transition model |
| **Transition error** | The model predicted the wrong consequence of a correctly executed action | Contract the trust envelope for the region and queue the transition for validated retraining |
| **Policy error** | The rollout policy selected unrealistic continuations beyond the first step | Revise or replace the rollout policy; transition accuracy is unaffected |
| **Value error** | Consequences were predicted correctly but ranked badly | Revise the scoring rule or evaluator; the world model is not implicated |
| **Binding error** | The executed action differed from the planned action | Repair the Step 1.2 parameter mapping and strengthen the execution diff |
| **Exogenous change** | An outside event altered the transition independently of the action | Update the belief state; do not penalise model accuracy |

**Required Outputs**:

- A residual record per decision-relevant field and an anomaly flag set for residuals beyond the 95th percentile.
- An attribution assignment per material divergence with supporting evidence.

**Quality Checkpoints**:

- Every material divergence carries exactly one attribution class; the label of undifferentiated plan failure is never used.
- Observation is captured before any subsequent planning in 100% of decision steps.
- Disputed attributions escalate rather than defaulting to transition error.
- Anomalous residuals are quarantined before entering training or evaluation data, with zero exceptions.

***

#### Step 6.2: Update the State, Model, and Trust Envelope

**Required Actions**:

- Append the real transition to the evaluation store with full lineage, including run identifier, version manifest, and attribution class.
- Update beliefs immediately from the observation, including hypothesis weights and resolved contradictions.
- Update model parameters online only where the learning procedure is controlled and bounded; otherwise queue the transition for offline retraining and validation.
- Adjust calibration, action-specific trust, and the operating envelope per the Step 2.2 contraction rule, quarantining suspicious traces and requiring regression testing before promotion.

**Required Outputs**:

- An appended transition record with complete lineage and an updated belief state.
- A trust envelope revision record and a quarantine register for anomalous traces.

**Quality Checkpoints**:

- Two consecutive transition errors in the same state region halve the permitted horizon for that region; three demote the region to abstention until re-validated.
- Zero unverified anomalies enter training data, since self-correction must never mean learning directly from every anomalous event.
- A promoted model passes at least 95% of the prior regression benchmark with no regression in tier 3 and 4 constraint recall; failure blocks promotion.
- Data lineage is complete for every stored transition, since transitions without lineage cannot be excluded later if their source proves compromised.

***

#### Step 6.3: Replan or Close the Run

**Required Actions**:

- Return to Step 3.2 and generate a new decision where the goal remains incomplete and no stop condition has fired.
- Rebase any reused branch on the newly observed state, discarding branches whose assumptions the observation invalidated.
- Close the run where success criteria, stop conditions, budget limits, or escalation rules are met, recording which terminator fired.
- Record the counterfactual comparison at closure, stating whether WMSP changed the action relative to a reactive baseline, whether that change improved the outcome, and whether the simulation cost was justified.

**Required Outputs**:

- A replanning record linking the new decision step to its parent, or a run closure record.
- A rebased branch set with invalidated branches marked as discarded.
- A counterfactual comparison against the reactive baseline for the closed run.

**Quality Checkpoints**:

- Zero branches are reused without rebasing on the observed state.
- Every closed run carries a counterfactual comparison, since without it a costly planner appears valuable merely by being present.
- The run terminates within its declared budgets; overruns are recorded as budget failures rather than absorbed silently.
- More than 10 consecutive replanning cycles without measurable progress triggers escalation.

## III. Implementation Guidance for AI Agents

AI agents operating WMSP must follow systematic protocols ensuring that simulated evidence is bounded by measured competence, that commitment is narrow, and that every prediction is answerable to the observation that follows it.

### A. Structured Execution Protocol

**Control Plane Components**:

1. **Planning Supervisor**: Owns the run, holds budgets and stop conditions, evaluates the trust gate, and authorises the transition from planning to execution.
2. **State Estimation Agent**: Normalises observations, maintains hypotheses, and computes staleness and distribution-shift scores. Holds read-only capabilities exclusively.
3. **Candidate Proposal Agents**: One agent per proposal method, each identified in the candidate register so proposal-method bias is measurable.
4. **Simulation Agent**: Generates rollouts. Holds no credential reaching a side-effecting interface, and its output is never rendered in real-tool response format.
5. **Evaluation and Challenge Agents**: Score trajectories, apply vetoes, generate adverse scenarios, and search for ranking-reversing counterexamples. Run on a different model family, checkpoint, or validator stack from the Simulation Agent.
6. **Execution Agent**: Performs the execution diff, applies safeguards, commits the tranche, and captures the raw response. It is the only component holding side-effecting capability.
7. **Divergence and Learning Agent**: Measures residuals, assigns attribution classes, updates the envelope, and manages quarantine and promotion.

**Workflow Execution Pattern**:

```
STATE: Decision_Step_Active
ACTIONS:
  1. Estimate belief state and compute staleness
  2. Evaluate trust gate and select mode
  3. Generate candidates, allocate budget, produce rollouts
  4. Challenge rollouts and admit surviving trajectories
  5. Score, select, and run execution diff
VALIDATION:
  - staleness_within_bound == TRUE
  - trust_mode_selected == EXACTLY_ONE
  - candidate_count BETWEEN 3 AND 7
  - surviving_trajectories_per_candidate >= 3
  - execution_diff_field_agreement == 1.00
TRANSITIONS:
  IF trust_mode == ABSTAIN THEN next_state = Escalate_To_Fallback
  IF all_validations_pass THEN next_state = Commit_Tranche
  IF execution_diff_mismatch THEN next_state = Rescore_Candidates
  ELSE next_state = Decision_Step_Error_Handling
```

**Three-Phase Action Pattern**:

1. **Simulate**: Generate and challenge trajectories with zero side effects under an isolated capability set.
2. **Verify**: Confirm precondition currency, execution diff agreement, and approval validity while the action is still uncommitted.
3. **Commit**: Execute the bounded tranche only after verification passes, then re-observe before any further action.

**Context and Capability Management Requirements**:

- Grant side-effecting capability to exactly one component, and only after verification reports agreement.
- Bound working context by summarising rejected trajectories to their rejection triggers rather than retaining full trajectory content.
- Persist the run manifest, candidate register, and trajectory summaries at every decision step, so a context reset resumes from the manifest rather than restarting the run.
- Record structured predictions, component scores, and evidence references rather than private reasoning traces, since the decision record must be auditable without storing hidden deliberation.

### B. Quality Assurance Checkpoints

**Checkpoint 1: Contract Completeness (After Phase 1)**

- **Automated Check**: Every decision-relevant outcome appears in the return obligations; every action parameter maps to exactly one interface field; at least one envelope exclusion is stated.
- **Agent Action on Pass**: Proceed to validation.
- **Agent Action on Failure**: Halt and return the unmapped parameters and uncovered outcomes.
- **Human Review Trigger**: If more than 20% of decision-relevant outcomes cannot be expressed as model return obligations.

**Checkpoint 2: Envelope Validity (After Phase 2)**

- **Automated Check**: Constraint recall at or above 0.95 for tier 4 and 0.80 for tier 3; calibration error at or below 0.10; a finite horizon limit established; out-of-envelope requests rejected in 100% of trials.
- **Agent Action on Pass**: Authorise runtime operation within the envelope.
- **Agent Action on Failure**: Remove the failing partition and operate in advisory mode for that region.
- **Human Review Trigger**: If tier 4 actions cannot be brought inside any validated envelope entry.

**Checkpoint 3: Gate Integrity (After Phase 3)**

- **Automated Check**: Exactly one trust mode selected with recorded condition values; staleness within bound for every decision-relevant field; hypothesis weights sum to 1.
- **Agent Action on Pass**: Proceed to candidate generation.
- **Agent Action on Failure**: Re-observe and re-run the gate rather than proceeding under the previous mode.
- **Human Review Trigger**: If the gate abstains on more than 30% of decision steps across a rolling window of 50 steps.

**Checkpoint 4: Rollout and Ranking Sufficiency (After Phase 5)**

- **Automated Check**: Sample counts match budget; every branch carries a terminator; every candidate retains at least 3 surviving and 1 adverse trajectory; execution diff agreement at 1.00; catastrophic-loss probability within the tier threshold; committed tranche at 1 action or one atomic bundle.
- **Agent Action on Pass**: Execute and capture the response.
- **Agent Action on Failure**: Halt before execution and regenerate only the deficient candidates.
- **Human Review Trigger**: Any tier 4 execution, any aggregate rejection rate above 40% after one regeneration pass, and any case where the robust leader differs from the mean-reward leader.

**Checkpoint 5: Loop Closure (After Phase 6)**

- **Automated Check**: Observation captured before further planning; every material divergence attributed to exactly one class; anomalies quarantined; counterfactual comparison recorded at closure.
- **Agent Action on Pass**: Replan or close the run.
- **Agent Action on Failure**: Block model updates and escalate the unattributed divergence.
- **Human Review Trigger**: If transition errors in a single region reach 3 within a rolling window of 20 decision steps.

### C. Error Handling and Troubleshooting

**Error Type 1: Model Exploitation**

- **Symptoms**: A candidate scores far above its peers, relies on an unusual action or parameter value, and its advantage collapses under model-variant swap; high-scoring plans fail at a rate exceeding the model's stated accuracy.
- **Diagnostic Steps**: Re-score the leader under each ensemble member separately; measure the distance from its parameters to the nearest validated training region; check whether the advantage persists when the evaluator is replaced.
- **Resolution Protocol**: Option A, penalise ensemble disagreement and distance from validated data until the anomalous advantage disappears. Option B, shorten the horizon and replan after every material observation, since exploitation grows with depth. Option C, remove the exploiting action class from the envelope and route it to the trusted baseline or fallback controller. Option D, raise the tier threshold so novel and irreversible actions require stricter evidence.
- **Escalation Trigger**: Three diagnoses within a rolling window of 50 decision steps suspend autonomous operation for the affected action class pending adversarial re-validation.

**Error Type 2: Compounding Rollout Error**

- **Symptoms**: Predictive quality degrades sharply with depth; deep branches dominate the ranking while shallow branches disagree; execution divergence correlates with the depth of the winning trajectory.
- **Diagnostic Steps**: Recompute the horizon curve on recent transitions; compare rankings at depth 1, depth 2, and the current maximum; measure whether deeper rollouts changed the selected action at all.
- **Resolution Protocol**: Option A, cap depth where the recomputed curve crosses the decision threshold. Option B, adopt short branched rollouts that preserve most of the lookahead benefit while limiting accumulated bias. Option C, replace deep simulation with a probe action resolving the uncertainty the depth was attempting to cover.
- **Escalation Trigger**: If depth reduction to 1 step still fails to bring divergence within the validation residual distribution, abstain in the affected region.

**Error Type 3: Binding and Execution Mismatch**

- **Symptoms**: The execution diff reports field disagreement; the executed payload differs from simulated parameters; outcomes fail despite correct consequence predictions.
- **Diagnostic Steps**: Compare the Step 1.2 parameter cross-reference against the current interface schema; check for interface version change since the manifest was frozen; verify that identifiers, amounts, targets, and permission scopes match exactly rather than approximately.
- **Resolution Protocol**: Option A, repair the parameter mapping and re-run the diff. Option B, freeze execution for the affected operation and re-validate the interface schema. Option C, where the interface changed mid-run, close the run and open a new one with a refreshed manifest.
- **Escalation Trigger**: Any tier 4 binding mismatch halts execution immediately and is never resolved by retry; the operation stays frozen until the mapping is re-verified.

**Error Type 4: Reality Gap Widening**

- **Symptoms**: Divergence rises across multiple regions at once; exogenous-change attributions increase; predictions remain internally consistent while systematically missing real outcomes.
- **Diagnostic Steps**: Separate numeric error from structural omission by checking whether the observed outcome type was representable in the contract at all; test whether interfaces, prices, policies, or counterpart behaviour changed since the validation set was collected.
- **Resolution Protocol**: Option A, refresh the validation set from recent transitions and recompute the envelope. Option B, apply conservative control by shortening horizons, widening uncertainty, and raising approval requirements. Option C, add the missing outcome type to the contract and re-enter Phase 1 for the affected decision. Option D, suspend WMSP for the decision and revert to the trusted baseline.
- **Escalation Trigger**: If the envelope cannot be re-established within 2 validation cycles, decommission WMSP for the region rather than operating on an unvalidated model.

**Error Type 5: Correlated Self-Confirmation**

- **Symptoms**: Simulator and evaluator agree at a rate exceeding their independently measured accuracies; disagreement statistics approach zero while execution divergence stays high.
- **Diagnostic Steps**: Check whether simulator and evaluator share a model family, checkpoint, prompt scaffold, or training corpus; measure agreement on a held-out set with known outcomes.
- **Resolution Protocol**: Option A, replace the evaluator with a different model family or a tool-backed validator. Option B, add an independent constraint checker able to veto regardless of the evaluator's score. Option C, route tier 3 and 4 decisions to independent review until independence is demonstrated.
- **Escalation Trigger**: Agreement above 0.95 on a held-out set where measured accuracy is below 0.85 renders the disagreement statistic uninformative and forces conservative mode until an independent evaluator is available.

**Error Type 6: Learning Contamination**

- **Symptoms**: Model quality degrades after an update; anomalous transitions appear in training data; regression tests fail on previously passing cases.
- **Diagnostic Steps**: Trace the lineage of transitions admitted since the last passing model; check the quarantine register for bypassed entries; test whether degradation localises to the regions the new transitions covered.
- **Resolution Protocol**: Option A, roll back to the last model passing the regression benchmark and re-quarantine the suspect batch. Option B, separate training, evaluation, and promotion datasets so a batch cannot influence its own acceptance. Option C, require independent review of every transition entering training for tier 3 and 4 action classes.
- **Escalation Trigger**: Any contaminated promotion halts autonomous model updates; promotion authority returns to human review and is never restored by retry alone.

**Troubleshooting Decision Tree**:

```
WMSP DECISION FAILS
├─ PREDICTION DEFECT
│   ├─ Wrong consequence, correct execution → Transition error: contract envelope, queue retraining
│   ├─ Quality collapses with depth → Compounding error: recompute horizon curve, cap depth
│   └─ Outcome type absent from contract → Reality gap: re-enter Phase 1 for the decision
├─ SEARCH DEFECT
│   ├─ Leader collapses under variant swap → Model exploitation: penalise disagreement, shorten horizon
│   ├─ Unrealistic continuations beyond step 1 → Policy error: revise rollout policy
│   └─ Ranking correct, choice wrong → Value error: revise scoring rule and vetoes
├─ COMMITMENT DEFECT
│   ├─ Execution diff mismatch → Binding error: repair mapping, HALT if tier 4
│   ├─ Tranche exceeded one atomic unit → Restore receding-horizon commitment
│   └─ Approval absent or expired → HALT, escalate to authoriser
├─ EVIDENCE DEFECT
│   ├─ Simulator and evaluator agree implausibly → Replace evaluator, add independent checker
│   ├─ State stale beyond bound → Re-observe before planning
│   └─ Hypotheses collapsed prematurely → Restore hypothesis set, re-run gate
└─ LEARNING DEFECT
    ├─ Anomaly entered training → HALT updates, roll back, re-quarantine
    └─ Regression failed on promotion → Block promotion, return to prior model
```

### D. Observability, Metrics, and Continuous Learning

**Observability Record Families**:

- **Run-level**: Run and environment identifiers, objective, success criteria, constraints, consequence tier, timestamps, owner, authorisation policy, budgets, horizon limits, random seeds, final status, and parent-child links created by replanning.
- **Model and configuration**: World-model type and version, training snapshot or simulator build, evaluator and rollout-policy versions, prompt identifiers, tool schemas, state encoder version, calibration set, envelope version, confidence thresholds, search algorithm, and pruning policy.
- **State and observation**: State identifier, observation timestamp, source and provenance, normalised features, missingness flags, hypothesis weights, staleness, distribution-shift score, and access-controlled links to raw evidence, with large or sensitive artifacts hashed and retained as redacted summaries.
- **Candidate and rollout**: Per candidate, the action class, exact simulated parameters, proposer, preconditions, feasibility result, and rejection reason. Per rollout, the parent state, action, sampled next state, event probabilities, reward and cost components, hazards, uncertainty, disagreement, depth, termination reason, seed, and compute cost.
- **Decision and execution**: Hard vetoes, component scores, risk adjustment, ranking, tie-break rule, selected action, confidence, approval identity, payload hash, idempotency key, timestamps, tool response, and whether execution matched the plan.
- **Feedback and learning**: Actual next state, observed outcome, residuals, event correctness, constraint outcome, attribution class, anomaly flag, whether the transition entered training or evaluation data, reviewer decision, trust adjustment, regression result, and promotion decision.

**Outcome Metrics**:

- **Task success rate**: Share of closed runs meeting stated success criteria (target: at or above 0.85 for tier 1 and 2 workloads).
- **Constraint violation rate**: Share of executed actions breaching a hard constraint, severe violations counted separately (target: at or below 0.01 overall, 0.000 severe).
- **Decision uplift**: Success or utility gain against a reactive, rules-only, or prior planner under matched tasks (target: at or above 0.05, below which WMSP is decommissioned for the workload).
- **Avoided-action rate**: Share of proposed real actions vetoed or replaced after simulation, sampled for review (target: at least 0.60 of the sample is useful prevention rather than needless obstruction).
- **Top-action regret**: Outcome gap between the chosen action and the best action identifiable after the fact (target: at or below 0.10 of achievable utility).

**Fidelity Metrics**:

- **One-step transition accuracy**: Agreement between predicted next-state fields and the next real observation (target: at or above 0.85 in every active envelope region).
- **Multi-step degradation**: Change in predictive quality per additional rollout step (target: horizon capped where degradation exceeds 2 times the one-step error).
- **Event calibration error**: Difference between predicted event probabilities and observed frequencies across at least 5 bins (target: at or below 0.10).
- **Action-ranking accuracy**: Agreement between simulated rankings and rankings from real outcomes (target: at or above 0.80 for tiers 3 and 4).
- **Model disagreement**: Dispersion across ensemble members, samples, or alternative simulators, reported rather than minimised (target: monitored, values above 0.30 forcing conservative mode).
- **Out-of-distribution action rate**: Share of considered or executed actions outside the validated support region (target: at or below 0.05 considered, 0.00 executed for tier 4).
- **Model exploitation rate**: Share of highly scored plans failing because they used a simulator defect (target: at or below 0.02).
- **Prediction-to-execution divergence**: Difference between simulated and actual action parameters (target: 0.00).

**Efficiency and Adaptation Metrics**:

- **Simulation efficiency**: Simulated transitions, tokens, wall-clock time, and compute cost per real action and per successful run (target: at or below 0.20 of expected loss avoided).
- **Real-interaction efficiency**: Real environment interactions per successful task (target: monotonic reduction against the reactive baseline).
- **Replan rate**: Share of decision steps followed by material replanning, interpreted alongside success and divergence rather than minimised in isolation (target: monitored, values above 0.60 triggering horizon review).
- **Abstention precision and recall**: Whether the system abstains on cases genuinely exceeding its competence while still acting on supported cases (target: precision at or above 0.80, recall at or above 0.90 for tier 4).
- **Recovery rate**: Share of detected divergences resolved without a hard failure (target: at or above 0.90).
- **Learning velocity**: Improvement in calibrated prediction or success per validated batch of new transitions (target: positive across any 5 consecutive batches, or the learning procedure is revised).

**Robustness Analysis Protocol**:

- Run repeated plans with controlled variation in random seed, model sample, prompt wording, state noise, candidate ordering, and evaluator, reporting action-selection stability and outcome variance.
- Treat high agreement as inconclusive where all runs share a model family, training corpus, or architecture, and include independent model families, adversarial cases, temporal holdouts, and live shadow evaluation before concluding stability.
- Use horizon curves to locate where error becomes unacceptable and reliability curves to test whether stated probabilities mean what they claim.
- Use counterfactual replay to ask whether a revised planner would have avoided historical failures, recording that logged data cannot reveal outcomes of actions never taken.
- Report every metric partitioned by environment, action class, consequence tier, horizon, model version, and distribution-shift band, since a single average conceals precisely the cases where WMSP is dangerous.

**Learning and Adaptation Mechanisms**:

- **Residual-driven revision**: Treat measured forecast error, not narrative quality, as the signal revising the model, and evaluate foresight by calibration and downstream decision quality rather than by how convincing its expression appears [30].
- **Rule extraction from mismatch**: Convert recurring prediction-observation mismatches into durable rules or memory entries usable without retraining parameters, and filter low-confidence foresight before it reaches the planner [29].
- **Dataset separation**: Keep training, evaluation, and promotion datasets disjoint so a batch cannot influence the decision to accept itself.
- **Envelope maintenance**: Recompute the trust envelope whenever the environment version changes, the model is promoted, or 500 new validated transitions accumulate, whichever comes first.
- **Baseline retention**: Maintain a trusted baseline policy and fallback controller at all times, verifying quarterly that the fallback still executes correctly.

## IV. Domain-Agnostic Application Guidance

This framework is designed for generalisability across domains in which actions have consequences that can be modelled and observed. Its phases do not depend on the environment being physical, digital, or organisational.

### 4.1 Domain Adaptation

**Industrial and physical operations**: The world model is typically a hybrid of mechanistic dynamics and a learned residual, synchronised with asset-specific observation history [14][16]. State estimation combines sensor history, maintenance records, operating load, and ambient conditions with explicit uncertainty about recently changed components. Hard constraints are safety limits on predicted physical quantities, and the envelope is partitioned by operating regime. Commitment is characteristically a partial control change such as a load reduction, followed by immediate observation and a decision whether to inspect, which turns a one-time verdict into receding-horizon control.

**Digital interface and transactional agents**: The world model predicts semantic state deltas rather than regenerating the whole interface: what changes, what remains invariant, which events fire, and which new observations become available. Action semantics are grounded in tool schemas, documentation, demonstrations, and execution traces. The pattern earns its cost at irreversibility boundaries such as purchases, permission changes, message dispatch, and deployment, where a read-only probe is cheap and a commit is not [17]. A three-zone policy applies: read-only observations execute directly, reversible writes use a short rollout plus verification, and irreversible or externally visible actions require deeper simulation, independent validation, and approval.

**Scientific and evaluation campaigns**: The world model predicts how each candidate experiment changes knowledge, risk exposure, budget, and the set of tests remaining available. Belief states are competing hypotheses about the system under study, and information gain is a first-class scoring criterion rather than a tie-break. Cheap, reversible, sharply discriminating experiments frequently dominate expensive open-ended ones on robust ranking even where the expensive option has higher mean value, because the discriminating test collapses hypothesis uncertainty before the costly commitment is made.

**Organisational and resource planning**: The world model predicts dependency resolution, counterpart response, and schedule consequences rather than physical dynamics. Uncertainty is dominated by other parties acting strategically, so adversarial challenge in Step 5.1 carries more weight than stochastic sampling. Constraint satisfaction and approval gates typically supply stronger guarantees than the transition model, so WMSP is appropriately restricted to comparing a small number of consequential commitments.

### 4.2 Composition With Neighbouring Planning Patterns

WMSP is one component of a planning system rather than a replacement for the others, and several neighbouring patterns superficially resemble lookahead without carrying an environment transition model. Table 4 names those most often confused with it and states the relationship each bears to WMSP.

**Table 4: Composition with neighbouring planning patterns**

| Pattern | What it contributes | Relationship to WMSP |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Meta-reasoning** | Decides whether to think longer, change strategy, verify, or stop | Governs rollout budget, horizon, model choice, and stop rule; WMSP returns evidence about candidate actions |
| **Hierarchical task decomposition** | Refines abstract tasks into executable methods using domain knowledge | Generates structured candidate plans; WMSP tests risky branches and selects among applicable methods |
| **Task orchestration** | Manages ownership, dependencies, queues, retries, resources, and lifecycle | Invokes WMSP as a decision procedure and schedules retraining; it is not itself a predictive model |
| **Goal decomposition** | Turns an objective into coherent subgoals and interfaces | Applied first where the objective is underspecified; WMSP evaluates whether actions advance the resulting subgoals |
| **Constraint satisfaction planning** | Finds assignments satisfying explicit constraints | Filters illegal candidates and validates rollout states; WMSP predicts uncertain transitions the solver cannot derive |
| **Scenario planning** | Constructs strategically distinct futures for deep uncertainty | Covers regime uncertainty at policy scale; WMSP performs action-conditioned lookahead within each scenario |
| **Plan-execute decoupling** | Separates plan construction from tool execution | Supplies the execution boundary, binding checks, and replan protocol inside which WMSP forecasts consequences |
| **Plan recitation** | Re-emits explicit plan state so long work resists drift | Records which cycle is active, what was simulated, what was authorised, and what observation is awaited; imagined outcomes are never recited as completed facts |

The defining test separating WMSP from branch-exploring reasoning methods is whether predicted external or task state changes mediate action selection [25]. Where branches are merely candidate reasoning traces with no environment transition model, the pattern is not WMSP and must not inherit its trust machinery [26][27].

### 4.3 Scale Adaptation

- **Single-decision scope**: Collapse Phase 4 to direct enumeration with 3 candidates and 5 samples each, retain the trust gate and execution diff in full, and skip search-policy selection.
- **Continuous-operation scope**: Apply the full six-phase loop with persistent envelope maintenance, drift monitoring, and recomputation every 500 validated transitions.
- **Campaign scope**: Nest WMSP inside goal decomposition and orchestration, apply it only at consequential commitments rather than at every step, and maintain hypothesis sets across runs.
- **Multi-agent scope**: Assign simulator, evaluator, and challenger to separate agents with separate model families, since the independence that separation is intended to provide is otherwise nominal.

## V. Limitations and Considerations

**Model exploitation is the dominant failure mode**: Optimisation pressure applied to an imperfect simulator reliably discovers plans succeeding only inside the model, and the risk grows with search intensity, rollout depth, action novelty, and evaluator narrowness. Mitigation requires layered defence rather than a single control: penalise ensemble disagreement and distance from validated data, limit horizon and replan after every material observation, train on the actions the planner is likely to consider rather than only on historical behaviour, search adversarially for high-score low-plausibility trajectories, compare model rankings against real outcomes on a safe shadow set, retain a trusted baseline and fallback controller, require stricter evidence for novel and irreversible actions, keep training, evaluation, and promotion datasets separate, monitor calibration by action type and horizon, and preserve independent veto authority.

**Error compounds with depth**: Learned and approximate models carry generalisation error accumulating across simulated steps [21], so a deep rollout is not a longer view of the same quality but a progressively less reliable one. Mitigation is to treat model-generated trajectories as a trade between cheap data and rising bias, to use short branched rollouts preserving most of the lookahead benefit [20], and to set the depth cap from the measured horizon curve rather than from available compute.

**The reality gap exceeds numeric error**: Interfaces change, counterparts behave strategically, sensors fail, policies and prices shift, and a simulator may omit institutional or ethical constraints entirely. Domain randomisation, ensemble modelling, online identification, conservative control, and frequent real observation reduce the gap and none eliminate it. Mitigation requires the structural admission that some outcome types are absent from the contract, detected by checking whether an observed outcome was representable at all before attributing its miss to numeric inaccuracy.

**Recent generative world models do not settle the fidelity question**: The period since 2024 has broadened the term world model from compact control models to large generative systems that learn action-controllable interactive environments from unlabelled video [22], generate responsive three-dimensional scenes from a single image prompt [23], and perform self-supervised video prediction in an abstract representation space paired with action-conditioned modelling for robot planning [24]. These results reinforce the architectural claim that prediction in a learned latent space can support planning without rendering every detail. They do not establish that a general generative model is a calibrated simulator for an arbitrary consequential decision, because a system can generate coherent futures while failing at conservation, object persistence, causal intervention, rare hazards, or exact tool semantics. Mitigation is to treat foundation world models as candidate predictive components subject to the same Phase 2 validation as any other model class, judged not by whether a rollout looks plausible but by whether it ranks actions and predicts constraint-relevant outcomes reliably inside the intended operating envelope.

**Correlated evaluation undermines the disagreement signal**: Where simulator and evaluator share a model family, checkpoint, prompt scaffold, or training corpus, their agreement carries far less evidence than its numeric value suggests, and the disagreement statistic driving the trust gate becomes uninformative. Mitigation is to source the evaluator from a different family or a tool-backed validator, to add an independent constraint checker with veto authority, and to treat measured agreement above 0.95 on a held-out set with accuracy below 0.85 as a defect rather than a result.

**Counterfactual outcomes are unobservable**: Logged data records what happened after actions taken and cannot reveal what would have happened after actions never taken, so decision uplift and top-action regret are estimated under assumptions rather than measured directly. Mitigation is shadow-mode operation and randomised evaluation where safe, with every uplift figure labelled by the comparison basis that produced it.

**Simulation is not authorisation**: A successful rollout is evidence about consequences and carries no permission, so actions requiring external approval, legal review, safety certification, or human consent remain gated regardless of predicted outcome quality. Mitigation is to bind the approval gate to consequence tier at the execution boundary in Step 2.3, where it cannot be traded against a score.

**Observability creates its own exposure**: The logging required to reproduce decisions, compute metrics, and audit authorisation accumulates sensitive state and evidence. Mitigation is to record structured predictions, component scores, and evidence references rather than private reasoning traces, to hash large or sensitive artifacts, and to place raw evidence behind access controls proportionate to its sensitivity.

**Cost is not self-justifying**: A planner adding latency and compute can appear valuable simply because it is present and its runs are recorded. Mitigation is the mandatory counterfactual comparison at run closure in Step 6.3 and the decommissioning rule removing WMSP from workloads where decision uplift falls below 0.05.

## VI. Conclusion and Summary

World-Model Simulation Planning earns its cost at the boundary where an action becomes hard to undo. Its value is not that simulated futures are true but that measured, bounded simulation makes real interaction safer and more efficient when its errors are quantified and its authority is constrained by those quantities. The framework above converts that principle into a sequence: specify what must be predicted before choosing a model, validate at the level that governs decisions, gate every decision against a trust envelope, generate and then attack candidate futures, commit narrowly, and let the observation that follows revise both the belief and the model.

The discipline is carried by three separations that a fluent system tends to erode. Simulation is separated from evaluation, so that disagreement is observable. Simulation is separated from execution, so that no imagined result reaches a production interface. Prediction is separated from authorisation, so that a good forecast never becomes a permission. Agents holding these separations under time pressure operate the pattern; agents collapsing them operate a narrative generator with an execution capability.

**Key Success Factors**:

- **Decision-level validation**: Model competence is measured on transition accuracy, calibration, constraint recall, and action ranking, never on surface plausibility.
- **Enforced trust envelope**: Authority is granted per state region, action class, and horizon by measurement, and contracts automatically when divergence appears.
- **Narrow commitment**: One action or one atomic bundle executes per observation, converting a fallible forecast into a sequence of verified decisions.
- **Attributed divergence**: Every prediction-observation gap is assigned to a named error class, because the correction depends entirely on the class.
- **Adversarial challenge**: Candidates are ranked on trajectories that survived perturbation, variant swap, and counterexample search, not on the modal future.
- **Justified cost**: Every closed run records whether simulation changed the action and whether that change improved the outcome.

By following this guide, AI agents can apply predictive lookahead to consequential decisions while keeping the authority of that lookahead bounded by what has actually been measured, and can retire the pattern from workloads where it does not demonstrably improve outcomes.
