# Current-state maturity, readiness domains and stakeholder adoption

## Maturity levels (Current maturity and Target maturity columns of the gap register)

| Level | Name | Evidence that places a control here |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Non-existent | No documented policy or procedure, no evidence of the control activity, no assigned responsibility |
| 1 | Initial / ad hoc | Informal, undocumented process; depends on individual effort; reactive; little evidence or tracking |
| 2 | Managed | Documented procedure; assigned owner; some monitoring; evidence collection started |
| 3 | Defined | Standardized across the organization; documentation and training material; regular monitoring with defined metrics; continuous evidence collection |
| 4 | Quantitatively managed | Quantitative metrics tracked over time; performance compared against benchmarks; data-driven decisions |
| 5 | Optimizing | Continuous improvement from metrics and feedback; automation applied; leading practices adopted |

## Readiness domains (rows of the readiness table, each scored 1 = Not ready to 5 = Fully ready)

1. Leadership and governance: executive sponsorship and visible commitment; governance structure for framework oversight; decision authority and escalation paths; link to strategic planning.
2. Infrastructure and resources: technology sufficient for the framework's requirements; tools for implementing and monitoring controls; facilities; workforce capacity for the implementation.
3. Human capital and skills: competencies the framework needs; training and knowledge transfer; staffing levels and role definitions; access to outside expertise.
4. Culture and change management: culture compatible with the framework's principles; change-management capability and experience; communication; sources of resistance and how they are handled.
5. Process and operational maturity: how documented and standardized processes are; quality management and continuous improvement; measurement and analytics capability; cross-functional coordination.
6. Regulatory and compliance positioning: understanding of obligations; existing compliance programs and their maturity; audit and assessment experience; relationships with regulators and auditors.

Name the critical gaps that would block implementation progress.

## Stakeholder adoption (only when the user supplies survey, interview or usage data)

Score each stakeholder group on three dimensions, 0-100% each:

- Knowledge: awareness that the framework exists and why; understanding of the requirements for their role; skills to carry them out.
- Attitude: belief in the framework's value; willingness to adopt its practices; commitment to its success.
- Practice: following the requirements in daily work; applying them consistently; framework embedded in standard procedures.

A typical threshold for satisfactory adoption is 70%. Groups below it need targeted intervention; name the intervention.
