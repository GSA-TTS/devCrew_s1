# Citation rules and verification checks

Use the citation rules while drafting, and the verification checks to fill the Verdict and Disposition columns of the claim ledger.

## Citation rules while drafting

- Create citations during evidence extraction and drafting, not as a final step.
- One citation may support several adjacent claims only when its exact span supports all of them; split a compound sentence when different sources support different clauses.
- Prefer a DOI, official document, filing accession number, standards permalink, repository commit or versioned documentation page over a search page or tracking link; do not cite a homepage, search page or whole book when a stable specific source exists.
- Record the page, section, figure, table, paragraph, timestamp or fragment anchor where possible.
- Do not cite a dead link without an archived copy or a plain no-longer-available note, and check that an archived copy is from the right date and version.
- Do not cite a secondary article as if it ran the underlying study or issued the underlying rule, and do not turn X says Y into Y is true when the source only establishes the statement.
- Do not cite a search-result snippet, abstract or preview as if the full text had been read.
- Do not invent titles, authors, dates, quotes, page numbers, DOIs, URLs or access status.

## Verification checks for each claim

Atomize the draft into claims and label each one material, nonmaterial, analysis, recommendation or quotation. Check cited and uncited claims: a citation's presence is not support. A claim passes when every check below holds:

1. The citation link resolves, the fetched title matches, and the first author or organization, date and version are correct. If the page cannot be fetched, the source cannot be verified.
2. The exact cited span entails the claim with all its qualifications, numbers, units, dates and scope.
3. A quotation matches character for character, apart from clearly marked omissions or editorial brackets, and carries an anchor when one is available.
4. The citation sits next to the claim it supports and does not appear to support a neighbouring unsupported claim.
5. The source is primary where primary evidence should exist, current enough for the claim's freshness window, accessible or flagged as paywalled, and independent where corroboration is claimed.

## Dispositions

- kept: the claim passed and stays as written.
- qualified: the claim was narrowed to what the span supports, or labelled single-source, self-reported or dependent corroboration.
- removed: the claim failed and could not be repaired; it stays in the ledger and leaves the report.

Each verification pass must support, narrow, remove or relabel a claim, correct a citation, add a missing anchor, fix a metadata mismatch or disclose a limitation; rewording for style alone does not count. Stop when all material claims pass and the remaining uncited content is clearly analysis, recommendation or common structure.
