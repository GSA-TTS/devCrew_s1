# AI elements for a nonprofit policy

Use these when the user asks for an AI use or AI governance policy, or when AI use falls within the scope of another policy (data protection, information security, research ethics, communications). Either write a dedicated AI policy or add AI sections to the existing policy; in both cases cover all seven elements.

| Element | What the policy must state |
|---|---|
| Mission-aligned purpose and scope | AI use must support the mission and values; which AI uses and systems are covered. |
| Ethical principles | Commitments to fairness, transparency, accountability, privacy and non-discrimination, including testing for and mitigating bias. |
| Governance roles | Oversight by a board committee or an AI lead trustee; responsibilities of management, IT, data protection and program leads. |
| Data protection and confidentiality | Which data may be processed by AI tools; a restriction on copying personal or sensitive data into public AI services; vendor due diligence before a tool is approved. |
| Human-in-the-loop decisions | Critical decisions (eligibility, service prioritization, HR decisions) are never fully automated and always have a named human reviewer who is accountable. |
| Transparency to stakeholders | Donors, beneficiaries and the public are told when AI is used in ways that materially affect them. |
| Incident response and reporting | How AI-related harms and near-misses are reported and handled, linked to the incident, safeguarding or data-breach procedures. |

## Implementation items specific to AI

Add these to the adoption checklist:

- Publish an approved AI tools list.
- Configure the technical safeguards the requirements call for in the approved tools.
- Give short do and do-not guidance for the organization's common scenarios, such as grant writing, donor outreach and case notes.
- Address informal AI use already happening (shadow use) that the context mapping found, so that the requirements and the guidance cover it.
