# Adoption checklist: validation, implementation and review

Write the checklist as tick-box items tailored to the policy and the organization. Cover the three groups below; name a role (not a person) for each item where the user's material allows.

## Validation and governance

- Brief the relevant board committee on the context, scope and key choices before the near-final draft goes to it, so its review focuses on material issues.
- Share the draft with affected staff, volunteers or partners for practical feedback on clarity and feasibility, using structured questions rather than open editing.
- Record key decisions, trade-offs and their rationale in a short decision log or cover note.
- Have the board or its delegated committee approve the policy at the level its classification needs, and name an executive owner who understands the responsibilities.
- For an AI or technology-heavy policy, consider an AI lead trustee or a small cross-functional committee to oversee implementation and emerging risks; avoid large, slow decision bodies.

## Implementation and enablement

- Communication and training: short briefings, onboarding modules or micro-learning by role, not one long presentation or email.
- Integration into processes and tools: build the requirements into forms, templates, checklists and systems (for example system permissions, an approved AI tools list, procurement workflows) so that compliance is the easiest path.
- Repository: one place where the current version is kept, with version control, search and indexing by role and topic.
- Feedback channels: a simple way for staff and volunteers to ask questions, suggest improvements, or flag where the policy conflicts with real practice.

## Review and adaptation

- Cadence: a baseline review, often every year or every two years, on a calendar owned by governance or compliance staff.
- Triggers for an earlier review: a new law or regulation, a major incident, a technology change (such as a new AI tool), or a significant organizational change (merger, new service, expansion to a new country).
- Evidence for revisions: incident reports, audit findings, beneficiary feedback and staff surveys showing where the policy is unclear, too burdensome or not followed.
- Lean change control: keep the template and structure stable, change only what is needed, and close each review with an updated version, a brief change log and refreshed implementation activities.
