# SOP review checklist: common mistakes and their fixes

Run every row against a draft. Report each failed row as a finding: the row, the section or step it affects, and the fix applied in the revised SOP.

| # | Mistake | How to see it in a draft | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Too long and complex | Long narrative paragraphs; many pages of prose; training or configuration content in the body | Break into modular SOPs or supporting work instructions; use numbered lists, tables and visuals; move job aids, screenshots and examples to appendices |
| 2 | Regulatory or policy requirements missing | A process required by regulation, contract or policy with no requirement named in References or in steps | Name the applicable requirement in References and add the controlling step; if the user has not said which requirement applies, flag it as a question rather than inventing one |
| 3 | Generic, one-size-fits-all text | Same text reused across sites or units; no local systems, laws or risk profile | Name the site, system and local requirements in Scope and steps, or split into site-specific SOPs |
| 4 | Vague language and undefined terms | "periodically", "regularly", "as needed", "as appropriate", "timely"; terms or acronyms not in Definitions | Replace with a frequency, condition or threshold; add each term to Definitions |
| 5 | Unclear obligation | "should" used for mandatory actions; mixed "must", "should", "may" without pattern | "must" for mandatory actions, "may" for permitted options, "should" only where there is real flexibility |
| 6 | Steps addressed to "you" or to named people | "You update your ticket"; personal names | Third person by role: "The Coordinator updates the ticket" |
| 7 | Passive or weak steps | "The form is checked"; steps that start with a noun phrase | Active voice with a strong verb: Verify, Record, Update, Notify, Escalate |
| 8 | Unclear roles and handoffs | Steps with no acting role; a change of owner with no stated item, format or acceptance criterion | Name the role on every step; at each handoff state who passes what to whom, in what format, with what acceptance criterion |
| 9 | Everything assigned to one senior role | One director or executive owns most steps or every approval | Spread steps by competency and authority; keep the senior role for approvals that need it |
| 10 | Idealized or obsolete process | Steps that end users say they skip or do differently; references to retired tools | Walk through with representative end users or observe the work, reconcile the differences, then rewrite to the real process |
| 11 | Inconsistent terminology | One role or system called by two names | Pick one name, define it, use it everywhere |
| 12 | Purpose full of procedure | Purpose longer than three sentences or containing steps | Cut Purpose to one to three sentences; move steps to section 6 |
| 13 | Stale document | No review date, no owner, revision history ending years ago | Assign an owner and a review interval; add a revision history row |
| 14 | Poor access and version control | Local or paper copies; no version or effective date in the header | Header with version and effective date; store in one central repository with controlled access; retire superseded versions |
