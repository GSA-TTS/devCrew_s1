# Source-venue catalog

Use this to name each source's venue and its own tier in the Sources column of the grading table, and to know which caveats to record in its Heuristic notes column. Each venue has a default tier; the caveats say what moves a specific source up or down from it. A corroborating-signal venue is marked signal in the Sources column.

| Venue (Sources column) | Default tier (Sources column) | Caveats (Heuristic notes column) |
|---|---|---|
| Vendor case-study page | Tier 2 or 3, by specificity | Named customer plus a measurable outcome pushes toward Tier 2; qualitative-only language pushes toward Tier 3. Check the publish or update date and whether the page is still live on the vendor's site; a quietly removed case study is itself a signal. |
| Earnings call transcript or investor material | Tier 1 for the fact that the statement was made | Verify the specifics independently: securities exposure deters outright fabrication but does not guarantee precision, or that in production means full scale. Search transcripts for agent, agentic and autonomous alongside domain terms. |
| Regulatory filing (annual and quarterly reports, current reports, proxy statements, sector regulator filings, official use-case inventories, procurement or contract-award records) | Tier 1 | The highest-value venue where it exists for the domain. For government claims, prioritize official AI use-case inventories and procurement or contract databases over general filings. |
| Industry analyst report | Tier 2 if the firm's own independent research; Tier 3 if commissioned by the vendor discussed | Check whether the report is independent research or a vendor-commissioned white paper carrying the same firm's branding; the two look alike. |
| Trade press (sector outlets) | Tier 3; Tier 2 with original reporting | Original interviews beyond the press release earn the Tier 2 upgrade. Sector beat reporters often keep longitudinal coverage, which makes trade press a good place for the still-active-a-year-later check. |
| Conference proceedings, keynotes and technical talks | Tier 2 | Named speaker, often more technically specific than marketing copy. Look for recordings or slides on the conference's own site, video platforms or vendor developer-relations channels. |
| Open-source project activity | Corroborating signal, not a claim tier | Commit velocity, adopted frameworks and dependency graphs corroborate a claimed architecture, but activity alone does not establish a production deployment. |
| Patent filing | Corroborating signal | Signals R&D investment and direction; a lag of 12 to 18 months or more between filing and publication makes it a forward-looking build-out signal, not confirmation of a current deployment. |
| Job posting | Corroborating signal, strong for early build-out | Hiring for agentic AI roles or specific framework skills shows real budget and can reveal activity before any announcement; growth in posting volume for a tool signals scaling. Not sufficient alone to confirm a maturity claim. |
| Social or professional post, employee blog | Tier 3 or 4 | Elevate only when the poster is a named, identifiable practitioner describing verifiable specifics. Never sufficient standalone corroboration for a headline finding. |

## Venues by domain

- Government: official use-case inventories and procurement or contract-award databases are a distinct high-value Tier 1 venue; each jurisdiction publishes some form of them.
- Finance: prioritize regulator-mandated disclosures and earnings calls.
- Energy and data centres: sector trade press and regulatory-filing equivalents (utility rate cases, grid-operator filings) carry more value than generic tech press.
