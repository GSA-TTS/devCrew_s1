# Item-writing rules for multiple-choice items

Use these rules to write, level and check multiple-choice items. An item has a stem (the question or problem), one key (the correct option) and distractors (the incorrect options).

## Item type

- Write multiple-choice items only.
- Do not write open stems (fill in the blank) or any other item type.

## Cognitive levels

Every item targets exactly one of three cognitive levels, and each item aligns to a single level of the blueprint. The level sets the rigor or difficulty of the item.

| Level | Cognitive demand (typical verbs) | Degree of critical thinking | Can the answer be found on one page of a reference? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Knowledge | recognize, recall, identify, define, list | None | Most likely |
| 2 Application | apply, choose, demonstrate, dramatize, employ, interpret, operate, practice, schedule, sketch, solve, write, determine, use, classify | Moderate | Somewhat likely |
| 3 Analysis | analyze, distinguish, discern, solve; also troubleshoot, assess, appraise, calculate, categorize, compare, contrast, criticize, differentiate, discriminate, examine, experiment, question, test | Higher | Not likely |

What each level tests:

- Knowledge: the candidate's familiarity with the domain's specifications, standards, vocabulary and components.
- Application: the candidate's ability to use a concept to act, for example to choose, determine or solve, with a moderate degree of critical thinking.
- Analysis: the ability to break material down into its component parts so that its organizational structure is understood. This includes identifying the parts, analysing the relationships between them and recognizing the organizational principles involved. Analysis sits above knowledge and application because it requires understanding both the content and the structural form of the material.

To assign a level:

1. Name the verb the item asks the candidate to perform and match it to a row of the table.
2. Rate the critical thinking the item demands: none, moderate or higher.
3. Apply the one-page test: if the answer can most likely be read off one page of a reference, the item is a knowledge item, whatever its verb.
4. Where the signals disagree (for example "solve", which appears at levels 2 and 3), let the critical-thinking rating and the one-page test decide.
5. Record one level per item. If an item needs two levels, split it into two items.

## Content standards

- Focus on important concepts: theories, concepts, standards, regulations, practices and procedures.
- Give each item a single focus.
- Keep items relevant to what is being assessed.
- Avoid ambiguous or subjective items; the key must be defensibly correct.
- Write items that challenge the most knowledgeable candidates.
- Test the candidate's ability to apply concepts and solve problems, not their reading comprehension.
- Evaluate the candidate fairly against the minimum qualifications; do not use items to raise the standard of the field or to enforce personal or biased preferences.
- Fairness: no item puts any group of candidates at a disadvantage.
- Word items clearly enough for non-native English speakers and so that they translate easily into other languages.
- Avoid qualifiers and quantifiers; where one is unavoidable, make sure more than one reference supports what the item evaluates.
- Support every item with a validated reference before it goes to review. Use references no older than 10 years, except standards and laws.
- Use only acronyms from the approved list, where one exists.

## Stem rules

- State the problem clearly. A poorly written stem fails to state the problem, carries irrelevant information or is too vague.
- Write positive stems. Negative stems confuse candidates and make the item hard to understand: do not use words such as no, not, least, except or worst.
- Ask for a solution, not a recommendation: phrase the stem so that the options are specific actions to take.
- Keep the stem complete; never leave a blank for the candidate to fill.

## Option rules

- Make all options similar in length.
- Construct options in parallel: the same means, verb tense and general grammatical structure across all options of an item.
- Do not let any option stand apart from the others, by length, wording, detail or form.
- Write options that each require taking a specific action.

## Distractor rules

- A distractor must seem correct to a candidate who lacks the knowledge the item tests.
- Make distractors plausible: a candidate cannot easily discount them as infeasible. Ideally they attract unqualified candidates who are only partially competent in the topic.
- Do not write tricky distractors; they are unfair.
- Do not write fictitious distractors (invented terms, products or methods).
- Keep distractors similar to the key in overall design and in the same category. For example, if the key is an access control model, the distractors are also access control models.
- A distractor may be technically accurate if it is impractical to implement in the situation the stem describes.
- Draw distractors from common errors and misconceptions seen in real practice.

## Item workflow

1. Draft the item against these rules and attach its validated reference.
2. Review: allot about four minutes per item. No item enters review without a validated reference.
3. Revise the item in response to review.
4. Approve the item only when it passes the checklist below.

## Checklist for each item

- [ ] Multiple choice, one key, no open stem.
- [ ] One cognitive level recorded, consistent with its verb, critical-thinking rating and one-page test.
- [ ] Single focus on an important, relevant concept; not ambiguous or subjective.
- [ ] Stem states the problem clearly, has no irrelevant information and no negative words (no, not, least, except, worst).
- [ ] Stem asks for a solution; options are specific actions.
- [ ] Options similar in length, parallel in structure, none standing apart.
- [ ] Distractors plausible, not tricky, not fictitious, same category as the key, drawn from real errors or misconceptions.
- [ ] Plain wording that translates easily; approved acronyms only; qualifiers backed by more than one reference.
- [ ] Validated reference attached, no older than 10 years unless it is a standard or law.
- [ ] Fair to every group of candidates.
