# Validation plan for a multiple-choice question set

Include this plan, filled in for the set, after the answer key. The aim is to show that difficulty comes from cognitive demand, not from ambiguity or flawed construction.

## 1. Expert review without the key

- Give the items to one or more subject experts without the answer key.
- An item needs revision if an expert cannot find the key, or finds more than one defensible answer.

## 2. Cognitive level verification

- Map the verb each stem requires to its intended level.
- Medium items must require apply, analyze, compare or differentiate.
- Hard items must require evaluate, justify, design or synthesize.
- Rewrite any item whose actual demand is lower than its label.

## 3. Pilot and item analysis

Give the items to a group of students like the intended audience, then check:

| Statistic | Target |
|---|---|
| Difficulty index (proportion correct), medium items | about 0.3 to 0.8 |
| Difficulty index, hard items | about 0.2 to 0.5 |
| Each distractor's selection rate | at least 5%, and attracting low performers |
| Discrimination (high performers vs low performers) | positive |

- Revise or replace any distractor chosen by fewer than 5% of students: it gives no diagnostic information.
- Revise any item with zero or negative discrimination.
- Confirm or drop each hypothesised misconception according to how often its distractor is chosen.

## 4. Rationale record

Keep, for each item, why each distractor represents its misconception and which cognitive process the stem targets, so revisions after the pilot keep the item's intent.
