# Paper skeleton and length budget

## Words per page (planning figures; confirm by rendering one test page)

| Rendering | Approx. words per page of pure text | 3-page body budget |
|---|---|---|
| Single-column article, 11 to 12 pt | 500 to 650 | 1,500 to 1,900 |
| IEEE two-column conference template, 10 pt | 850 to 1,000 | 2,400 to 2,800 |

Headings, a table and an abstract each consume space. A one-table, single-column, 3-page paper plans for about 1,800 words of body text including the abstract. The reference list is not counted. For the IEEE two-column template, scale every section budget below by 1.4.

## Default skeleton (single column, about 1,800 words)

| # | Section | Words | Purpose | Citations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Title and Abstract | 120 to 140 | Problem, time window, method in one clause, three headline findings, one-line outlook | 0 |
| 1 | Introduction and scope | 240 to 260 | And, But, Therefore: what the field assumed, what breaks that assumption, what this paper does. Time window, research questions, what is out of scope | 5 to 8 |
| 2 | Method | 120 to 150 | The five elements below | 2 to 4 |
| 3 | State of the art | 900 to 980 | Subsections follow the field's own structure, not the sources. Each subsection: what is settled, then what is being standardized, then what the empirical evidence says | 18 to 28 |
| 4 | Contested points and evidence gaps | 190 to 220 | 3 to 5 named disagreements, each with both sides cited; 3 to 6 named gaps, each stated as: no fetched primary source establishes X | 6 to 10 |
| 5 | Open problems and outlook | 110 to 140 | What must be true for the field to converge; what a reader should build against now | 2 to 4 |
| - | References | not counted | IEEE numbered, ordered by first appearance | - |

Rules of thumb: about one citation per 40 to 60 words of body; at most one table, and only where the cell structure itself is the finding (it costs about 150 words of budget); no figures unless the template gives them for free; no subsection shorter than 80 words (merge instead).

## Method paragraph (PRISMA-lite)

In the Method section's word budget, state:

1. The corpus: what it was built from, its date range and its cut-off date.
2. The search: the query families and venues used to acquire the sources, one clause each, and the counts identified, screened and included. When the user's material does not state them, say that the corpus was supplied and the search counts are not stated.
3. The inclusion rule: the tier policy and the selection rubric, one sentence each.
4. The handling rule for contested points and for unverifiable values (reported as gaps, marked n.a.).
5. The verification rule: what was re-checked against the source text and the date the references were resolved.
