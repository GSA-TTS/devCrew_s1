# Writing the comments

## Major comment fields

Each major comment gives, in this order:

1. Location: page and line, section, figure or panel, table, equation, appendix, supplement, or dataset, repository, commit or code version.
2. Observation: what the manuscript says or shows.
3. Issue: the threat to validity, inference, reproducibility, governance, interpretation or contribution.
4. Why it matters: the consequence for the affected claim.
5. Affected claim: the Claim ID from the claim–evidence map.
6. Evidence or standard: the reporting standard, check result or source that supports the concern.
7. Minimum acceptable remedy.
8. Alternative acceptable remedy, when one is feasible.
9. Severity: Invalidating, Major uncertainty, Repairable reporting gap, Minor ambiguity or Optional strengthening opportunity.

## Major-issue categories

A finding in one of these categories is a major comment.

- Conceptual: problem not defined or bounded; framework absent or inappropriate; prior-evidence premise inaccurate or selective; question or hypothesis not evaluable; core constructs undefined or mismeasured; primary objective or outcome unclear; confirmatory and exploratory work conflated; gap rhetorical or nonmaterial; contribution does not address the gap; claim exceeds design or evidence scope.
- Design and methods: design and question mismatch; invalid causal, predictive or diagnostic inference; inadequate sampling, selection or attrition accounting; wrong experimental unit or pseudoreplication; missing or weak comparator or control; allocation, blinding or contamination defect; invalid or unreliable central measurement; material confounding, leakage or missing-data bias; insufficient method detail or artifact access; undisclosed protocol, outcome or analysis deviation.
- Data and analysis: unclear or unauthorized provenance or governance; dataset overlap, leakage or training contamination; unreported selection, cleaning or exclusion; invalid missing-data, outlier or preprocessing treatment; model, design and data mismatch; unhandled dependence, confounding, multiplicity or censoring; missing uncertainty, diagnostics or denominators; nonindependent or inadequate validation; ungrounded or incoherent qualitative claims; unsupported mixed-method meta-inference; essential code, data or artifacts inaccessible without adequate justification.
- Results and displays: missing primary, registered or null result; unreported exclusions, deviations, attrition or missingness; material inconsistency across text, abstract, tables, figures or supplement; incorrect denominator, unit, statistical value or uncertainty display; misleading visual encoding or omitted distribution; inadequate legend or source data; image adjustment, duplication, cropping, compositing or selection concern; invalid or unexplained equation, proof or complexity claim; pseudocode and implementation mismatch; central result unreproducible from the supplied data, methods or artifacts.

## Minor comments

Each gives the location and the bounded fix. Typical minor comments: define a term, acronym, replicate or error bar; state a boundary condition, threshold rationale, calibration schedule or theorem assumption; add a qualifier to a claim; correct a citation characterization; specify a model, version, catalog number, package, seed or accession; clarify recruitment date and site or which roles were blinded; add the sample size or denominator to a legend or table note; correct a unit or axis title; standardize decimal places; improve colour contrast; state whether an image is representative; define an algorithm's stopping criterion; link a supplement section to the main method.

## Author-facing tone

Identify the claimed contribution fairly; state strengths; explain overclaiming precisely; request bounded, actionable corrections; distinguish required from optional citation suggestions. Venue fit, priority judgements and the recommendation stay out of the author-facing parts.

## Reference request record

For each requested reference: its number in the References part; the location in the manuscript; the claim or gap affected; the scholarly rationale; necessity (required, recommended or optional); any own-interest connection. Each References entry is a complete bibliographic reference.

## Confidential comments to the editor

Topics that belong here: venue-fit and priority assessment where requested; a fundamental premise, design or contribution-logic defect; a candidate redundant or duplicate publication or undisclosed related work; suspected undisclosed protocol deviation or outcome switching; repeated or material unexplained numerical inconsistencies; suspected image duplication or manipulation; potential unauthorized data use, consent or license breach; possible training-data or benchmark contamination; irremediable lack of data, code or materials needed to assess a central claim; need for statistical, methodological, ethics, security, data-governance, image-integrity, formal-methods, artifact or domain specialist review; the reviewer's expertise, access or confidence limits. State facts and locations; do not assert intent.

## Quality controls for the whole review

- The synopsis is fair enough that the authors would recognize their work.
- Evidence is kept apart from impression, and claims are not confused with results or methods.
- A reporting gap is not called scientific invalidity on its own.
- Possible misconduct is not asserted as fact.
- Criticism stays within competence.
- Confidential concerns are kept out of the author-facing parts.
