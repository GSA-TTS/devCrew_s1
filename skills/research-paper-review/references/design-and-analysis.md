# Design-and-methods and data-and-analysis passes

## Design frame

- Identify the declared design and check the methods match it; note hybrid or multi-study designs, the phase (discovery, development, validation, confirmation, deployment), primary versus secondary analyses, and the intended inference (descriptive, associational, predictive, causal, mechanistic, interpretive, evaluative).
- Record the question elements: target population or system, exposure or intervention, comparator or counterfactual, outcome, time horizon, setting, intended inference.
- Design requirements by question type: descriptive (defined population, appropriate sampling, clear measurement); associational (valid measurement, confounder reasoning, temporal clarity); predictive (development and validation split, relevant metric, calibration, external evaluation where claimed); causal (defensible counterfactual or comparator, temporal ordering, confounding control, assignment logic); mechanistic (discriminating manipulations or measurements, alternatives considered); diagnostic (target condition, index test, reference standard, participant spectrum, blinding, timing); qualitative (coherent epistemology, context, sampling, collection, analytic method); theoretical (definitions, assumptions, derivation or proof, counterexamples and boundaries).

## Design-and-methods review points

- Design fitness: design, timing, controls, comparators, baselines and considered alternatives can support the intended inference; conclusions stay within design capability.
- Sampling: target, source and study population or system; sampling frame and its coverage; recruitment or data acquisition; eligibility and its justification; selection and survivorship pathways; representativeness and transferability limits; sample-size rationale or information power; flow, attrition and nonresponse with the risk of attrition bias.
- Experimental rigor: experimental unit and nested or repeated structure (pseudoreplication); randomization or allocation method, implementation and concealment; who was blinded and at which stage; positive, negative and technical controls; replicate type and independence; operational variation (batch, site, operator, order); manipulation checks; authentication of cell lines, reagents, instruments, datasets and software.
- Measurement: construct to measure mapping; validity evidence for instruments, assays or sensors; reliability and calibration; measurement error and its consequences; outcome definitions and hierarchy; proxy or surrogate adequacy; inter-rater or annotation process and agreement.
- Bias control: a bias map for the central claim covering confounding (selection of confounders by theory, measured before exposure, not a mediator or collider), selection, leakage and contamination, carryover, expectancy and observer effects, endogeneity and reverse causality, missing-data mechanisms, and researcher degrees of freedom with selective reporting.
- Procedural reproducibility: procedures, parameters, versions, environment, timing, sequence and implementation fidelity; required supplementary content and its accessibility; data, code and materials availability; protocol deviations, their disclosure and impact.
- Protocol fidelity: whether registration or preregistration is expected; artifacts located and their timing; protocol to manuscript concordance and outcome fidelity; legitimacy and reporting of deviations; labelling of exploratory analyses and discipline in confirmatory claims.

## Article-type checkpoints

- Randomized trial: allocation, concealment, masking; flow and attrition; primary outcome and sample-size basis; intervention fidelity and harms; protocol and registration concordance.
- Observational study: target population, frame, eligibility, selection; confounder theory and measurement; temporal order, missingness, sensitivity; bias and selection limitations.
- AI, ML or computational study: dataset provenance, license and consent; train, validation and test partitioning; leakage and contamination prevention; fair baselines and tuning budget; hyperparameters, seeds and compute; external, out-of-distribution or temporal validation; code, data and model availability.
- Qualitative study: methodological tradition and reflexivity; sampling rationale and information power; collection context; coding and analysis process; trustworthiness, triangulation and negative cases; transferability boundaries.
- Animal or laboratory study: experimental unit and replicate type; randomization, blinding and controls; sample size and exclusions; biological variables and resource authentication; welfare and protocol disclosure; ARRIVE alignment.
- Method maturity and allowed claims: proof of concept supports feasibility only; internal validation supports preliminary performance; external validation supports validity under the tested contexts; an implementation study supports operational, clinical or policy consequence; real-world benefit or harm claims need deployment evidence.

## Data-and-analysis review points

- Provenance and governance: source and owner, collection dates and setting, license and permitted use, consent and access governance, identifiers, version and chain of custody, known limitations; duplicate records and training or benchmark overlap at the correct entity level, with performance claims bounded where contamination cannot be ruled out.
- Preparation: an auditable pipeline; cleaning rules; inclusion and exclusion decisions with denominators; missingness inventory, mechanism and handling; outlier rules and their consequence; transformations; label source and annotation quality; feature selection. Leakage pathways: outcome information in predictors, future information at baseline, test data used in preprocessing or tuning, participant, site or time overlap across partitions, encoding, imputation, normalization or feature selection fitted on all data. Controls: split before preprocessing, grouped split at the correct unit, nested cross-validation for tuning, frozen holdout or external test set.
- Statistical and computational analysis: analysis fits the design, outcome and data structure; assumptions checked substantively; estimands and effect sizes with uncertainty; significance kept apart from practical or clinical significance, and a null or uncertain result not treated as proof of no effect without regard to precision; planned and realized sample adequacy; multiplicity sources and control; model specification and covariate selection; dependence, clustering and repeated measures; missing data, censoring and competing events; diagnostics and performance evaluation including calibration; analysis populations and denominators; for Bayesian work, model, priors, computation, convergence, posterior checks and sensitivity.
- Robustness: a matrix of central results against sensitivity analyses, alternative specifications, negative and positive controls, placebo or falsification tests, subgroup and interaction analyses, external validation, stress and boundary tests, and dependence on arbitrary choices.
- Qualitative analysis: coherence of stance and methodology; sampling logic and adequacy; transparency of collection; coding framework and coder procedure; reflexivity and positionality; credibility, dependability and transferability techniques fitted to the methodology; disconfirming cases and grounding of claims in data.
- Mixed-method integration: rationale and necessity of mixing; sequence and priority; integration point and mechanism; handling of concordance and discordance; justification of meta-inferences.
- Traceability audit for each central finding: source data and version; eligibility and denominator; preparation path; measurement or label provenance; model; assumptions and diagnostics; robustness or validation evidence; the exact output, table or figure; the allowed conclusion.
- Reproducibility level: review evaluability; computational reproducibility; independent replication feasibility; restricted-data reproducibility under controlled access; nonreproducible with justified limitation.
- Reporting completeness: exact n and denominators; distinct versus repeated observations; test or model specification; covariates; assumptions and corrections; effect size and uncertainty; multiple-testing treatment; data and code availability; provenance and version; cleaning, exclusions and missingness; partitioning; deviations and exploratory analyses; robustness and negative findings; access restrictions.

## Ratings

| Dimension | Scale |
|---|---|
| Design fitness | Strong: design identifies and supports the intended inference. Adequate: generally fit with bounded limitations. Weak: partial mismatch, weak comparator or insufficient temporal or validation logic. Defective: design cannot support the central inference or contribution. |
| Sampling | Strong: target, source, sample, acquisition, eligibility, size, flow and limits transparent and fit. Adequate: credible with bounded coverage or reporting gaps. Weak: substantial selection, representativeness or attrition uncertainty. Defective: sampling or selection prevents credible central inference. |
| Experimental rigor | Strong: correct unit, allocation, masking, controls, replication, variation control and resource quality assurance. Adequate: sound core design with disclosed or mitigated limitations. Weak: preventable bias, independence or replication limitations. Defective: central result invalidated by allocation, control, unit or contamination problem. |
| Measurement | Strong: construct-valid, reliable, calibrated, clearly defined, bias-aware. Adequate: credible with bounded known limitations. Weak: uncertain validity, reliability or proxy fit. Defective: central construct or outcome invalidly or inconsistently measured. |
| Bias control | Strong: major pathways anticipated, prevented or handled, and bounded. Adequate: principal risks handled with residual uncertainty. Weak: material pathways inadequately measured or addressed. Defective: unaddressed bias or confounding invalidates the central result. |
| Procedural reproducibility | Strong: procedures, resources, parameters, context and artifacts allow independent evaluation and repetition. Adequate: core method reproducible with bounded missing detail. Weak: material omissions obstruct reproduction or evaluation. Defective: central procedures or artifacts unavailable or too underspecified to evaluate the claims. |
| Protocol fidelity | Strong: timely registration or protocol, clear mapping, justified transparent deviations, exploration labelled. Adequate: minor transparent discrepancies. Weak: incomplete access or unclear deviations. Defective: undisclosed outcome or analysis changes materially threaten credibility. |
| Provenance and governance | Strong: source, ownership, consent, version, lineage, use rights, limitations and overlap risks transparent. Adequate: credible with bounded documentation gaps. Weak: material uncertainty about lineage, access, overlap or fitness. Defective: unclear or unauthorized data use, unreconstructable provenance or contamination undermines central claims. |
| Data preparation | Strong: auditable, justified, partition-safe, consistent, transparent. Adequate: appropriate with bounded documentation or sensitivity gaps. Weak: material uncertainty about cleaning, selection, missingness, labels or leakage. Defective: outcome or test information contaminates preparation, or unreported selection invalidates the central analysis. |
| Statistical analysis | Strong: matches design, data and question, with assumptions, uncertainty, dependence, multiplicity, missingness, diagnostics and interpretation handled transparently. Adequate: credible with bounded sensitivity or reporting issues. Weak: material specification, assumption, dependence, multiplicity or uncertainty gap. Defective: invalid model, leakage, denominator ambiguity, unhandled dependence or selective reporting prevents support of the central conclusion. |
| Robustness | Strong: central findings survive relevant plausible alternatives and are validated as the claims require. Adequate: key checks support the result with bounded uncertainty. Weak: important assumptions or choices untested, or instability unaddressed. Defective: conclusion contingent on an unreported or arbitrary choice, nonindependent validation or a failed falsification test. |
| Qualitative analysis | Strong: coherent methodology, transparent collection, coding and reflexivity, credible grounding, bounded interpretation. Adequate: limited transparency or trustworthiness gaps. Weak: unclear process, thin grounding, or unaddressed positionality or negative evidence. Defective: methodology and question mismatch, or claims not grounded in the data. |
| Mixed-method integration | Strong: clear rationale, coherent sequence and priority, explicit integration, transparent concordance and discordance, justified meta-inferences. Adequate: partial but meaningful integration. Weak: strands merely colocated or integration implicit. Defective: meta-inference unsupported by a strand, or incompatible designs combined without justification. |

## Pass-specific cautions

- Do not demand randomization or blinding where infeasible or inappropriate; require a rationale and alternative bias controls.
- Do not require a particular method when several valid designs or analyses could answer the question.
- A large sample does not protect against systematic bias; a sophisticated model is not evidence of valid design; adjustment or prediction performance alone does not establish causality.
- Do not confuse technical repeats, repeated measurements and independent replicates; do not conflate cross-validation with independent external validation.
- A low P value, high predictive score or large sample is not sufficient evidence of validity; robustness to trivial specifications is not robustness to core assumptions.
- Do not require open data where consent, privacy, security, law, sovereignty or legitimate proprietary constraints preclude it; require a transparent restriction and a qualified-access path instead.
- Do not require every qualitative study to use inter-rater agreement, member checking or triangulation; judge fit to the methodology. Two strands in one paper are not by themselves an integrated mixed-methods study.
- Do not require preregistration retroactively; require transparent exploratory labelling instead.
