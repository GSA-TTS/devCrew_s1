# Assessment criteria, lesson components and adaptation design

## Benefits and drawbacks: Criterion column

Benefits:
- Demonstrated effectiveness: outcomes against stated objectives, size of effect (small, moderate, large), time to realize.
- Efficiency advantages: cost-effectiveness against alternatives; financial, human and technical resources; administrative burden on government; compliance cost on regulated parties or beneficiaries.
- Positive spillovers and co-benefits: positive effects in other domains, synergy with complementary policies, capacity building, innovation and learning.
- Equity benefits: groups that benefit most (note whether intended), inclusion mechanisms, reduced disparities.
- Implementation strengths: political acceptability and stakeholder support, administrative feasibility with existing capacity, adaptability provisions, long-term sustainability.

Drawbacks:
- Effectiveness limitations: objectives not or partly achieved, perverse incentives or behavioural adaptation, evidence of failure.
- Efficiency concerns: high cost against benefit, strain on budgets, duplication or fragmentation, opportunity cost.
- Negative unintended consequences: harm to non-target groups, adverse spillovers, long-term risks and path dependency, capture or corruption vulnerability.
- Equity concerns: disproportionate burden on disadvantaged groups, regressive effects, access barriers, discrimination or exclusion.
- Implementation challenges: political opposition, administrative complexity and coordination failure, capacity deficits, resistance from implementers or regulated parties, rigidity.

Where the evidence allows, disaggregate equity effects by group (for example income, geography, gender, age, disability) and say whether the policy reduces, keeps or widens disparities.

## Lessons: columns

- Lesson (what): the principle or finding in one or two sentences.
- Why: the causal logic and the pattern or theory that supports it.
- Where it applies: the contexts where it holds and where it does not, and the conditions it needs.
- Evidence: the jurisdictions that show it, with citations, the strength of that evidence, and any case where it did not hold.
- Implications for target: what it means for policy design in the target, the local adaptations needed, implementation considerations.
- Category: policy design (mechanisms, scope and targeting, resource allocation, sequencing); implementation (administrative structures, capacity, stakeholder engagement, outreach); evaluation and learning (monitoring indicators, evaluation methods, data, feedback); political and contextual (coalitions, timing and windows of opportunity, framing, risk management).

## Adaptation pathways: target context dimensions

- Political: governance and decision processes, current priorities, feasibility and windows of opportunity, champions, opponents and veto players.
- Institutional: administrative capacity and structures, legal and regulatory framework, bureaucratic culture, inter-agency coordination.
- Economic: resources and budget limits, development level, market structure and private sector role, available financing.
- Social: demographics, cultural values and norms, civil society capacity, past experience with similar policies.
- Technical: infrastructure and technology, data systems, workforce skills, knowledge networks.

## Mismatch levels

- Fatal: the approach is not viable in the target.
- Significant: major adaptations are required.
- Moderate: minor modifications are needed.
- Minimal: the approach transfers readily.

## Adaptation design types

- Direct Transfer: adopt with minimal changes; rare, only when contexts are highly similar.
- Inspired Adaptation: keep the core principles and redesign implementation for the target.
- Hybridization: combine features from several jurisdictions.
- Contextualized Innovation: use the comparison to inform a locally designed solution.

## Pathway contents

- Modifications: which instruments to keep, modify, replace or add; scope and targeting changes; sequencing and phasing; resources sized to the target's budget and capacity; governance suited to local structures.
- Implementation pathway: preconditions before adoption; pilot or phased rollout; capacity building; stakeholder engagement and coalition building; risk mitigation for the challenges found.
- Monitoring provisions: indicators for progress and outcomes; decision points for course correction; ways to keep learning; sunset or review provisions.
- Say what is adapted from which jurisdiction and why.
