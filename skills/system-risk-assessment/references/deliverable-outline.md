# Deliverable outline: sections, per-risk content and writing principles

## Sections, in order

1. Executive summary: key findings and overall risk profile, critical risks needing immediate attention, high-level recommendations, alignment with risk appetite.
2. Introduction and scope: objectives and purpose, system description and boundary, stakeholders identified, scope and limitations.
3. Methodology: the framework followed (ISO 31000, NIST or another the user names), identification techniques used, analysis method (qualitative, quantitative or semi-quantitative), evaluation criteria and rating scales, data sources and evidence.
4. System characterization: description, component inventory and architecture, operating context, dependencies.
5. Risk identification results: risks by category, scenarios, threats and vulnerabilities, dependency risks.
6. Risk analysis results: likelihood and impact per risk across the impact dimensions, existing controls and their effectiveness, exposure scores or values, root causes of priority risks.
7. Risk evaluation and prioritization: appetite, tolerance and thresholds used, acceptability decisions, the prioritized list, a heatmap or matrix.
8. Risk treatment plans: strategy per priority risk, actions with owners and timelines, resources, residual risk, cost-benefit notes.
9. Monitoring and review: key risk indicators and thresholds, monitoring approach and frequency, review schedule, escalation.
10. Conclusions and recommendations: overall risk posture, key recommendations, implementation priorities, next steps.
11. Appendices: the full risk register, technique outputs (FMEA, fault tree, bowtie) where used, stakeholder input records, supporting data, glossary.

For a rapid assessment, or a simple, lower-risk system, a streamlined form is allowed, but every essential element stays: the boundary, the criteria, the rated register, the treatment plans, and the scope limitations with a recommended follow-up.

## Register columns

ID; category; risk (source or cause, event, consequence); component or dependency affected; existing controls and their effectiveness; likelihood; impact; risk level or score; acceptability; priority tier; treatment option; residual level; source of the evidence (user document, user statement, or assumption).

## Per-risk characterization

Description with sources and consequences; context; likelihood and impact results; uncertainty and confidence; existing controls; evaluation result and priority; treatment recommendation; expected residual risk.

## Writing principles

- Transparency: methods, assumptions and uncertainties are explained.
- Clarity: plain language for the audience; define jargon.
- Consistency: the same criteria and terms for every risk.
- Reasonableness: each conclusion follows from the evidence given.

## Audience emphasis

- Executive leadership: high-level summary, strategic implications, resources needed.
- Operational management: risk profiles, control requirements, implementation guidance.
- Technical teams: technical analysis, specific vulnerabilities, remediation detail.
- Audit and compliance: methodology, evidence, compliance mapping.
