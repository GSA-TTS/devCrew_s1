# Textbook chapter standards

Quality standards for a technical certification textbook and its chapters. Apply them when drafting, revising or checking a chapter, and when assembling the book's front and back matter. Each section ends with checks you can answer yes or no.

## 1. Objectives and exam alignment

Book level:

- State broad competencies for the whole book, graded by the Revised Bloom's Taxonomy levels: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create.
- Align them with the official certification exam content outline (the exam blueprint) and its job-analysis standards, using the most recent version of the outline.
- Write them so the book serves both individual self-study and adoption as a course text.

Chapter level:

- Give each chapter 3 to 8 learning objectives.
- Make each objective SMART: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.
- Begin each with an action verb that matches its cognitive level (for example "list" for Remember, "configure" for Apply, "diagnose" for Analyze, "justify" for Evaluate, "design" for Create).
- State them from the learner's side, as what the learner will be able to do by the end of the chapter.

Mapping:

- Keep an explicit map that links each chapter section to its learning objectives, its assessment items and the exam domains it covers. Present it as an alignment matrix that an adopting institution can file.
- Fix the prerequisite sequence: every chapter names what it builds on, and nothing is used before it is taught.

Checks:

- [ ] The chapter has 3 to 8 objectives, each measurable and led by a level-appropriate verb.
- [ ] Every objective maps to at least one exam domain, and every exam domain the chapter claims is covered by an objective.
- [ ] Every objective is taught in the content and tested by at least one assessment item.
- [ ] Prerequisites are named and come earlier in the book.

## 2. Chapter structure

Modular design:

- Make each chapter a self-contained module, a learning chunk of roughly one to two weeks of study.
- Use the same sequence in every chapter: Overview, Learning Objectives, Content, Key Concepts, Assessment.
- Give the content a clear hierarchy: primary concepts, then subtopics, then supporting detail.
- Name chapters and sections in parallel form with one naming convention throughout.

Scaffolding:

- Present foundational concepts before advanced applications.
- Raise complexity step by step within a chapter and from chapter to chapter.
- Link related concepts across chapters explicitly (by chapter and section).
- Add a short prerequisite review for learners who may have gaps.

Real-world relevance:

- Ground each concept in an authentic workplace scenario or job task.
- Cover the relevant industry standards, best practices and emerging technologies.
- Show the concept applied in a realistic situation that reflects actual job demands, and tie it to the professional competency it builds.

Terminology:

- Define every technical term in the text, in plain language, at first use.
- Use terminology consistent with the official certification standards.
- Give the origin or context of a complex term where it aids understanding.
- Enter each term in the glossary with a cross-reference to where it is defined.

End-of-chapter assessment:

- Match practice questions to the official exam format and difficulty, using multiple-choice, scenario-based and performance-based types.
- Explain why each correct answer is right and why each wrong option is wrong.
- Organise questions by domain or topic, and end the chapter with review questions of increasing complexity.

Checks:

- [ ] The five-part sequence is present and in order.
- [ ] Headings are parallel and follow the book's naming convention.
- [ ] No concept is used before it is introduced.
- [ ] Every new technical term is defined at first use and appears in the glossary.
- [ ] At least one workplace scenario or job task grounds the main concepts.
- [ ] Review questions mirror the exam format and explain every option.

## 3. Accuracy and currency

Currency:

- Set a review cycle, annually or at each exam update, to verify the content.
- Monitor changes to the certification exam and update content to match.
- Track emerging technologies and trends in the field.
- Validate content with subject matter experts and industry practitioners.
- Show the publication date and the next review date in the material.

Exam updates:

- Keep a process for rapid updates when the exam changes.
- Use version control and mark updated sections clearly.
- Tell adopting institutions about updates before they need them.
- Keep backward compatibility where possible during a transition, and write a transition guide when the exam version changes.

Technical accuracy:

- Have certified professionals validate the technical content.
- Have subject matter experts review lab procedures and simulations.
- Test every procedure, command and technical step in a real environment.
- Confirm that software versions and screenshots match current releases.
- Add an accuracy disclaimer wherever a technology version affects the instructions.

Checks:

- [ ] Every command and procedure has been run in a real environment, or is flagged as untested.
- [ ] Software versions and screenshots are named and current.
- [ ] Content matches the current exam outline version, and that version is recorded.
- [ ] Publication and next review dates are present.
- [ ] Version-dependent instructions carry a disclaimer.

## 4. Accessibility and inclusive design

Conformance (WCAG 2.1 Level AA for all digital content):

- Give alt text to every image, diagram and screenshot.
- Use colour combinations with a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1.
- Make every interactive element work by keyboard.
- Make PDFs and documents fully searchable and accessible.

Universal Design for Learning:

- Offer multiple means of representation: text, visuals, audio, interactive elements.
- Offer multiple means of engagement: choice of activities and relevance to diverse backgrounds.
- Support multiple means of expression: writing, discussion, projects, presentations.
- Keep the structure modular so learners can vary sequence and pace.
- Allow flexible formatting (adjustable fonts, spacing, backgrounds).

Features:

- Captions and transcripts for every video.
- Text descriptions for complex diagrams and processes.
- Semantic structure (real headings, lists and tables) in digital material.
- Tables with proper header cells and clear row and column relationships.
- A glossary with pronunciation guides for technical terms.

Checks:

- [ ] Every figure has alt text, and every complex figure also has a text description.
- [ ] Colour contrast is at least 4.5:1, and no meaning depends on colour alone.
- [ ] Every table has header cells.
- [ ] Heading levels nest without skips.
- [ ] Every video has captions and a transcript.

## 5. Front and back matter

Front matter:

- A preface stating the book's purpose, its target audience and how to use the book.
- An overview of the certification exam: structure, format and testing requirements.
- A description of how the book maps to the official exam objectives and domains.
- Guidance on study strategies, time management and exam preparation.
- The prerequisite knowledge or background the reader should have.
- If AI tools were used in development, a disclosure: which tools, for what purposes (editing, graphics, content generation), which content is human-created and which AI-assisted, how accuracy was validated, and how readers can report AI-related errors.

Navigation and reference:

- A table of contents showing the chapter and section hierarchy.
- A detailed index with cross-references and related-term suggestions.
- A glossary with definitions, acronyms and pronunciation guides.
- Reference cards or visual bookmarks for frequently needed information.
- Chapter summaries that highlight the key takeaways.

Back matter and appendices:

- Appendices with reference material, configuration examples and a command reference.
- Additional resources: websites, organisations, professional associations, study groups.
- Recommendations for further reading and advanced study.
- Troubleshooting guides listing common issues with their solutions.
- Quick reference guides and checklists for exam preparation.

Checks:

- [ ] The preface names purpose, audience and how to use the book.
- [ ] The exam overview and the objective-to-chapter map are present.
- [ ] Table of contents, index and glossary are present and agree with the chapters.
- [ ] Each chapter has a summary of key takeaways.
- [ ] An AI-use disclosure is present whenever AI tools were used.
