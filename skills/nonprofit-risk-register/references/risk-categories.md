# Risk categories: what to review, how to find risks, typical mitigations

Use one section per category. Each section gives the material to review, the identification technique, the risks that commonly appear, the mitigations that answer them, and the extra columns or outputs that category needs.

## 1. Operational

- Review: the program's operational dependencies: staff, facilities, equipment and vehicles, supplies, and partners.
- Identify: single-point-of-failure analysis. For each dependency ask "What if it is lost?" (the coordinator leaves, the vehicle breaks down, the main supply partner withdraws, the facility becomes unavailable).
- Common risks and mitigations:
  - Staff turnover: cross-training, written procedures, competitive pay and retention measures.
  - Vehicle or equipment breakdown: preventive maintenance, a backup rental agreement, roadside assistance, an emergency repair fund.
  - Key supply partner fails: a written agreement (MOU), diversified sources, an emergency purchasing budget, active relationship management.
  - Facility loss: lease protection, backup facilities identified in advance, a portable or temporary option.
- Extra output: a contingency plan for every risk rated Critical or High impact.

## 2. Financial

- Review: program-level financial risks the user already has (funder concentration, cash-flow timing mismatches, cost inflation, turnover costs), then add organization-level risks.
- Organization-level risks and mitigations:
  - Audit findings: a pre-audit review by a CPA, internal controls, an audit preparation checklist, a board finance committee.
  - Fraud or misappropriation: segregation of duties, dual signatures above a set amount, monthly reconciliations, written expense policies.
  - Tax compliance failure: an experienced nonprofit CPA, an annual compliance review, board review of the annual information return (Form 990 in the US).
  - Insurance coverage gaps: a broker review and an annual review; name each coverage type with its limit and annual cost (general liability, vehicle, directors and officers, cyber liability, workers compensation, as the activities require).
- Integrate: where a mitigation for a program-level risk already exists, reference it and add only what is missing; do not list the same action twice.
- Extra output: compliance costs for year 1 and the ongoing annual amount, where the user gives or confirms the figures.

## 3. Legal and compliance

- Review: the program's activities, and from them list the laws and rules that apply: service-specific health and safety rules (for example food safety), nonprofit compliance, employment law (wage and hour classification, overtime, workers compensation, anti-discrimination, workplace safety), data privacy, vehicle operation.
- Assess: give each law or rule a compliance status: Compliant, Partial or Gap.
- Identify: each Partial or Gap status becomes a risk (for example a safety incident, an unpaid-overtime claim, a data breach, a vehicle accident).
- Mitigate with a compliance checklist per area:
  - Service safety SOP: monitoring logs, expiry or quality checks, stock rotation, inspection readiness.
  - Employment handbook: wage and hour, overtime, leave, anti-discrimination, safety, grievance procedure.
  - Privacy policy: data collected, its use and protection, participant rights, encryption, access controls.
  - Vehicle safety: driver screening (motor vehicle records), safe operation training, a maintenance contract, commercial auto insurance.
- Extra output: a compliance budget (year 1 and ongoing).

## 4. Strategic

- Review: the strategic plan and market analysis, and list the strategic assumptions: target population size, growth targets, partnership strategy, revenue mix, competitive position, mission.
- Identify: one "what if" scenario per assumption: the need shrinks, competition increases, a key partnership is lost, the mission drifts, funder priorities shift.
- Assess: impact, likelihood, and a time horizon (for example 1-3 years, 3-5 years).
- Common mitigations, aligned with the strategic plan:
  - Need shrinks: continuous needs assessment, flexible program design, geographic expansion, focus on outcomes.
  - Competition increases: coordination through a coalition, a clear differentiator, partnership rather than competition.
  - Partnership loss: written agreements with each partner, backup partners or sites, an emergency budget.
  - Mission drift: board review of every new program against the mission, annual strategic planning, stakeholder input.
  - Funder priorities shift: revenue diversification with a target cap on the largest funder's share, outcome data that shows impact, advocacy, a list of alternative funders.
- Extra output: a monitoring indicator for each strategic risk.

## 5. Program delivery

- Review: the program design and its monitoring and evaluation framework, and list the quality, safety and outcome requirements (service quality, education or service content, customer service, participant and volunteer safety, data privacy, outcome targets).
- Identify: risks such as quality failures, an ineffective service or curriculum, participant dissatisfaction, missing an outcome target, participant injury, volunteer injury, a data breach.
- Assess: impact, likelihood, and which outcome target the risk threatens.
- Common mitigations (quality assurance procedures):
  - Service quality: a safety and quality SOP, a complaint process, rapid response to complaints.
  - Ineffective education or service: an evidence-based curriculum, cultural and language adaptation, a pilot test, trained educators.
  - Participant dissatisfaction: customer service training, shorter wait times, culturally appropriate services, feedback mechanisms.
  - Outcome failure: attendance supports (incentives, childcare, convenient times), engaging content, continuous improvement from monthly data review.
  - Participant or volunteer injury: site safety checks, weather protocols, safe lifting training and equipment, supervision, incident reporting, liability and workers compensation coverage.
  - Data breach: encrypted storage, access controls, staff security training, a privacy policy, cyber liability insurance.
- Extra output: a monitoring metric for each program-delivery risk (satisfaction surveys, outcome measurement, incident tracking).
